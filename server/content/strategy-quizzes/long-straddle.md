# Quiz

1. When is a long straddle most appropriate?
   - You expect the stock to stay in a narrow range and want to collect premium while nothing happens to it
   - [x] You expect a large price swing but don't know the direction, such as before earnings or a regulatory decision
   - You are certain the stock will rise and want the maximum leverage on the move from a single option
   - You want to collect premium income from a stock that you expect to stay quiet through the next few weeks
   > A straddle is a bet on the size of the move, not its direction.

2. What is the trade-off of a long straddle?
   - You collect two premiums, but you face unlimited risk if the stock moves a large amount either way
   - You must pick the direction correctly, because only one of the two options can ever finish in the money
   - [x] You pay for two options, so a quiet, range-bound stock is a loss on both legs
   - The profit is capped at the premium paid, because one of the two options always expires worthless
   > The cost of both options must be earned back by a large enough move.

3. How is a long straddle built?
   - Sell a call and a put at the same strike and expiration, collecting both of the premiums up front
   - Buy an out-of-the-money call and put at different strikes
   - Buy a call and sell a put at the same strike and expiration, so that the position behaves like the stock
   - [x] Buy an at-the-money call and an at-the-money put with the same strike and expiration
   > Both options are bought at the same strike, usually at the money.

4. A stock barely moves after earnings and settles near the strike. What happens to a long straddle?
   - [x] Both options lose most of their value, so most of the premium paid is lost
   - The straddle earns its maximum profit
   - The straddle is assigned shares, because the options finish at the money on the expiration date
   - Only the put loses value, because the call gains from the small rise that happens after earnings
   > Without a large move, neither leg gains enough to offset the cost.
