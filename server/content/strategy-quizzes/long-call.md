# Quiz

1. Which situation best fits buying a call option?
   - You expect the stock to stay flat and want to collect income
   - [x] You expect a sizable rally within a defined time and want to risk only a small, known amount
   - You already own the shares and want to protect them against a sharp decline without selling them
   - You expect a large move but have no view on direction and want to profit if it happens either way
   > A long call is a bullish, limited-risk way to take part in a rally.

2. What is the main appeal of a long call compared with buying the shares outright?
   - It pays you a steady income while you wait for the stock to move, which the shares themselves do not
   - It never loses any value as time passes, since the contract is guaranteed to hold its price
   - [x] The loss is limited to the premium while the profit potential keeps growing as the stock rises
   - It makes money whether the stock rises or falls, so being right about direction is unnecessary
   > Capital at risk is capped at the premium, while the upside is uncapped.

3. How is a long call typically set up?
   - Sell one call well above the current price and hope it expires worthless so that you keep the entire premium
   - Buy a call that expires within a few days so that time decay works strongly in your favor
   - Buy one call and one put at the same strike so that you profit from a large move in either direction
   - [x] Buy one call at or slightly above the current price, with enough time to expiration for the view to play out
   > Enough time matters because the stock must move before the option's time value runs out.

4. A trader buys a call, and at expiration the stock finishes at or below the strike. What happens?
   - [x] The call expires worthless and the trader loses only the premium paid
   - The trader must buy 100 shares at the strike price, which causes a loss on top of the premium
   - The trader owes the seller the difference between the strike price and the stock price
   - The trader keeps the premium because the call is out of the money
   > The most a call buyer can lose is the premium paid; the obligation to deliver belongs to the seller.
