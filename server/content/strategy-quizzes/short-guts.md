# Quiz

1. What distinguishes a short guts position from a short strangle?
   - Both options start out of the money, so the credit is smaller and the risk is correspondingly lower overall
   - It uses the same strike for both options, so it is identical to a short straddle apart from the price
   - [x] Both short options start in the money, so the credit is larger but the risk is correspondingly higher
   - It has limited risk, because the in-the-money options cannot lose more than the credit received for them
   > Guts uses in-the-money options, unlike a strangle's out-of-the-money options.

2. When might a trader use a short guts position?
   - They expect a large move in either direction and want to be paid up front for the volatility that follows
   - They want limited, known risk and are willing to collect a smaller credit for the safety of that position
   - They want to profit from a rise in volatility and expect the options to become more valuable to the seller
   - [x] They expect the stock to stay range-bound and want a larger credit than a short straddle, accepting higher risk
   > It is a higher-conviction variation of a short volatility position.

3. How is a short guts position built?
   - [x] Sell an in-the-money call and an in-the-money put with different strikes that straddle the current price
   - Buy an in-the-money call and put with different strikes that straddle the current price, paying a debit
   - Sell a call and a put at the same out-of-the-money strike, which gives a smaller credit with less risk
   - Sell a call and buy a put at different strikes that straddle the price, to reduce the overall exposure
   > The call strike is below the price and the put strike is above it.

4. A sharp move occurs after a short guts position is opened. What happens?
   - The trader keeps the entire credit, because in-the-money options are always worth less after a sharp move
   - [x] Losses result, just as with any short volatility position, and they can be large
   - The trader earns unlimited profit, because the short options gain value as the stock moves further away
   - The loss is limited to a small premium
   > A short guts position has unlimited risk if the stock moves far.
