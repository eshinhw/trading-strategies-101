# Quiz

1. {#q1} What problem does securitization solve for a lender?
   - [x] Loans tie up its capital for years, which limits how much new lending it can do
   - Loans never get repaid
   - Borrowers cannot find lenders
   - Lenders are not allowed to charge interest
   > Selling the loans, in the form of securities, gives the lender cash back right away so it can lend again.

2. {#q2} In securitization, where do the payments on the securities come from?
   - The government's budget
   - [x] The combined cash flows from the pool of loans
   - New investors' deposits only
   - The lender's profits
   > Investors are paid from the interest and principal that borrowers pay on the pooled loans.

3. {#q3} Why can splitting a pool into tranches help investors?
   - It guarantees every tranche pays the same
   - It removes all risk
   - [x] Different investors can choose a safer or riskier slice that fits what they want
   - It makes the loans disappear
   > Tranches have different risk and return, so investors can pick the level of risk that suits them.

4. {#q4} What does originate-to-distribute mean?
   - Lenders keep every loan until maturity
   - [x] Lenders make loans with the intention of selling them to investors
   - Investors make loans directly to the government
   - Borrowers distribute their debts to friends
   > The lender originates loans and then distributes them to investors by securitizing.

5. {#q5} What is a possible downside of securitization?
   - It makes credit unavailable
   - [x] If lenders sell nearly every loan, they may take less care about the quality of each loan
   - It makes loans cheaper for everyone with no risk
   - It requires lenders to hold all of the risk
   > When risk is passed on, the incentive to screen borrowers carefully can weaken, as seen in the 2007 to 2009 crisis.

6. {#calc1} [calc] A bank securitizes $240 million of mortgages and recycles the cash into new loans three times in total, earning a 0.4% fee each time. What is the total lending and the total fee income?
   - $240 million of lending and $0.96 million of fees
   - $720 million of lending and $0.96 million of fees
   - [x] $720 million of lending and $2.88 million of fees
   - $720 million of lending and $28.8 million of fees
   > Three rounds of $240M is $720M, and a 0.4% fee on each is 3 × $0.96M = $2.88M.

7. {#calc2} [calc] A bank earns a 0.35% fee securitizing $180 million, repeated in 4 rounds. What are the total fees?
   - [x] $2.52 million
   - $630,000
   - $25.2 million
   - $0.63 million
   > Each round earns 0.35% × $180M = $630,000, and 4 rounds earn $2.52 million.

8. {#bk1} [calc] A pool has 100 loans of $1 million each. Each has a 2% chance of default and 60% loss given default. What is the expected loss on the pool?
   - $2 million
   - [x] $1.2 million
   - $0.6 million
   - $12 million
   > Expected defaults are 100 × 2% = 2 loans. Expected loss = 2 × $1 million × 60% = $1.2 million.

9. {#bk2} [calc] A $500 million pool is divided so the senior tranche attaches at 20% subordination. How much of pool loss can occur before senior investors lose anything?
   - $20 million
   - $400 million
   - $500 million
   - [x] $100 million
   > Subordinated tranches below the senior tranche total 20% × $500 million = $100 million of protection.
