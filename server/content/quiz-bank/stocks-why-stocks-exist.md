# Quiz

1. {#q1} How does raising money by selling shares differ from borrowing?
   - Shares must be repaid with fixed interest on a set date
   - [x] The company has no obligation to repay shareholders, who are paid only if the business does well
   - Shareholders are guaranteed a fixed return
   - Selling shares is only possible for private companies
   > Equity raises money without a repayment date or fixed interest, and investors accept that their return depends on the company's success.

2. {#q2} What is the primary market?
   - The market where investors trade shares with each other
   - A market reserved for the largest companies
   - [x] The market where a company first sells new shares, such as in an IPO, and receives the money
   - A market for borrowing and lending
   > In the primary market the company itself sells new shares and raises capital, while later trading happens in the secondary market.

3. {#q3} Why does the secondary market matter to a company that doesn't receive money from those trades?
   - It doesn't, since the company is not involved
   - It lets the company set its own share price
   - [x] Investors are more willing to buy shares they can easily sell later, which makes it cheaper for the company to raise money
   - It guarantees that the share price will rise
   > A liquid secondary market makes shares more attractive at issue, which lowers the company's cost of raising capital.

4. {#q4} What does limited liability mean for a shareholder?
   - The shareholder is responsible for all of the company's debts
   - [x] The most the shareholder can lose is the amount invested, even if the company fails
   - The shareholder can never lose money
   - The shareholder must repay creditors from personal assets
   > Limited liability caps a shareholder's loss at their investment, which makes it easier for many people to take part in funding a business.

5. {#q5} [calc] A company sells 10 million new shares at 20 dollars each. How much does it raise?
   - 20 million dollars
   - 100 million dollars
   - [x] 200 million dollars
   - 2 billion dollars
   > Ten million shares at 20 dollars each raises 10 million × 20 = 200 million dollars.

6. {#bk1} [calc] A company sells 10 million new shares in an IPO at $18 each. How much does it raise before fees?
   - $18 million
   - $1.8 billion
   - [x] $180 million
   - $10 million
   > 10,000,000 × $18 = $180,000,000.

7. {#bk2} [calc] A company raises $180 million in an IPO and pays underwriting fees of 7%. How much does it keep?
   - [x] $167.4 million
   - $172.8 million
   - $12.6 million
   - $173 million
   > Fees = 7% × $180 million = $12.6 million. Net = $180 million − $12.6 million = $167.4 million.

8. {#bk3} [calc] A founder owns 4 million of a company's 8 million shares. The company then issues 2 million new shares to outside investors. What percentage does the founder own afterward?
   - 50%
   - 25%
   - [x] 40%
   - 20%
   > The share count rises to 10 million. 4 million / 10 million = 40%.

9. {#bk4} [calc] A company needs $10 million. Borrowing would cost 5% interest every year no matter how the business does. Selling shares requires no fixed payment. How much fixed interest does the borrowing route commit the company to each year?
   - $5 million
   - [x] $500,000
   - $50,000
   - $0
   > 5% × $10 million = $500,000 a year, owed regardless of profits. Equity carries no such fixed payment.

10. {#bk5} Why might a company raise money by selling shares instead of borrowing?
   - Shares always cost less than any loan
   - Shareholders can demand their money back at any time
   - Selling shares does not change who owns the company
   - [x] Shares carry no fixed payment that must be made whatever the business earns
   > A loan means fixed interest and repayment regardless of results. Equity investors share in the outcome instead.

11. {#bk6} What is the difference between the primary and the secondary market?
   - The primary market is for bonds and the secondary market is for stocks
   - [x] In the primary market the company sells new shares to raise money, while in the secondary market investors trade shares with each other
   - In the primary market investors trade with each other, while in the secondary market the company sells new shares
   - Both markets deliver money to the company on every trade
   > An IPO raises money for the company in the primary market. After that, trades happen between investors and do not raise new money for the company.
