# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A company reports earnings of $1.32 per share against an expected $1.20. The standard deviation of past surprises is $0.06. What is the standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) score?
   - 10.0
   - [x] 2.0
   - 0.12
   - 0.5
   > SUE = (actual − expected) / standard deviation of surprises = ($1.32 − $1.20) / $0.06 = 2.0.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A company reports earnings of $1.32 per share against an expected $1.20. What is the percentage surprise?
   - 12%
   - 9.1%
   - 0.12%
   - [x] 10%
   > Surprise = ($1.32 − $1.20) / $1.20 = 10%.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A stock is held for 60 days after a positive surprise and earns 4.5%. Annualized on a simple 365-day basis, what is that return?
   - 4.5%
   - [x] 27.4%
   - 15.0%
   - 54.8%
   > 4.5% × 365 / 60 = 27.4%.

4. {#bk4} [calc] A stock jumps 4% on earnings day and then drifts up another 3% over the next 40 days. What annualized rate does that drift represent (simple, 365 days)?
   - 3.0%
   - 11.0%
   - [x] 27.4%
   - 24.0%
   > 3% × 365 / 40 = 27.4%.

5. {#bk5} [calc] A strategy buys the top 10% of 1,000 stocks ranked by earnings surprise. How many stocks does it buy?
   - [x] 100
   - 10
   - 900
   - 1,000
   > 10% × 1,000 = 100 stocks.

6. {#bk6} Where is post-earnings drift typically strongest?
   - In the largest, most heavily covered stocks
   - Only in stocks that pay dividends
   - [x] In smaller, less-covered stocks, where information is absorbed more slowly
   - In stocks that do not report earnings
   > Fewer analysts and less trading slow the market's reaction to news.
