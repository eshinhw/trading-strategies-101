# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A firm must hedge $2,400,000 of exposure using futures on a related asset. The correlation between the two prices is 0.85, the exposure's volatility is 20% and the futures' volatility is 25%. Each futures contract has a notional value of $150,000. How many contracts (rounded to the nearest whole contract) minimize risk?
   - 16
   - [x] 11
   - 14
   - 20
   > Minimum-variance hedge ratio = correlation × exposure volatility / futures volatility = 0.85 × 20% / 25% = 0.680. Contracts = 0.680 × $2,400,000 / $150,000 = 10.9, about 11.

4. {#bk4} [calc] A cross hedge has a correlation of 0.8 between the exposure and the hedging future. What share of the exposure's price variance does the hedge remove?
   - 80%
   - 20%
   - [x] 64%
   - 89%
   > The variance reduction equals the correlation squared: 0.8² = 0.64, so 64% of the variance is removed.
