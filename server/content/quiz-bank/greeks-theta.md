# Quiz

1. {#q1} What does theta measure?
   - The effect of volatility
   - [x] The change in an option's price from one day passing, other things equal
   - The change from a 1 dollar stock move
   - The effect of interest rates
   > Theta is time decay per day.

2. {#q2} What sign does theta have for a long option?
   - Positive
   - [x] Negative, since the option loses value as time passes
   - Zero
   - It depends only on the strike
   > Long options lose time value each day, so their theta is negative.

3. {#q3} When does time decay speed up?
   - Right after the option is bought
   - [x] As expiration approaches, especially for at-the-money options
   - Never, it is constant
   - Only on weekends
   > Decay accelerates close to expiration for at-the-money options.

4. {#q4} Who benefits from time decay?
   - Option buyers
   - [x] Option sellers, who collect it if the stock stays calm
   - Stockholders only
   - No one
   > A seller's position has positive theta, but also carries negative gamma risk.

5. {#q5} [calc] A call has a theta of −0.06. About how much does one contract lose per day, all else equal?
   - 0.06 dollars
   - [x] About 6 dollars
   - 60 dollars
   - 600 dollars
   > A per-share theta of −0.06 is −0.06 × 100 = −6 dollars per contract per day.

6. {#bk1} [calc] An option has a theta of −$0.05 per share per day. A trader holds 5 contracts (100 shares each) for 10 days with no other change. What is the time-decay loss?
   - [x] $250
   - $25
   - $2.50
   - $500
   > Decay = $0.05 × 100 shares × 5 contracts × 10 days = $250.

7. {#bk2} [calc] A short option position earns $0.04 per share a day from time decay. What does the trader earn on 100 shares over 7 days?
   - $4
   - $280
   - [x] $28
   - $0.28
   > Earnings = $0.04 × 100 × 7 = $28.
