# Quiz

1. {#q1} Why do commodity producers and consumers care so much about price swings?
   - Commodity prices never change
   - [x] Their profits can depend as much on the commodity's price as on how well they run the business
   - They are required by law to hold commodities
   - They only trade commodities for fun
   > A farmer or an airline can't control the price of its key input or output, so large swings directly affect profit.

2. {#q2} What is price discovery?
   - A regulator setting prices
   - Finding the cheapest supplier
   - [x] Many buyers and sellers in one market reflect what they know in a single visible price
   - A company deciding its own price
   > A central market aggregates information from many participants into a transparent reference price.

3. {#q3} How does a producer use commodity futures to hedge?
   - It buys futures to increase its price risk
   - [x] It sells futures to lock in the price of what it will produce
   - It stops producing
   - It avoids any contracts
   > A producer that sells futures fixes the price it will receive, reducing its exposure to falling prices.

4. {#q4} What role do speculators play in commodity markets?
   - They set government policy
   - They only trade physical commodities
   - [x] They take the other side of hedgers' trades, which adds liquidity and lowers the cost of hedging
   - They prevent anyone from hedging
   > Speculators bear risk in hope of profit, which makes it easier for hedgers to find a counterparty.

5. {#q5} Why might an airline buy oil-related futures?
   - To speculate on rising profits
   - To own physical barrels of oil
   - [x] To lock in much of its fuel cost for the year, removing price risk from its budget
   - To avoid ever buying fuel
   > Fuel is a large cost for an airline, so locking in its price makes the company's costs more predictable.

6. {#calc1} [calc] An airline burns 60 million gallons of fuel a year and hedges half at $2.50. Fuel rises to $3.10. What is its extra cost after the hedge gain?
   - [x] $18 million
   - $36 million
   - $30 million
   - $9 million
   > The extra cost on all fuel is 60M × $0.60 = $36M. The hedge on 30M gallons gains 30M × $0.60 = $18M, so the net extra cost is $18M.

7. {#bk1} [calc] An airline needs 2 million gallons of fuel and hedges at $2.90 a gallon. Fuel later trades at $3.30. How much does the hedge save compared with buying at the market?
   - $80,000
   - $8,000,000
   - [x] $800,000
   - $400,000
   > The airline pays $2.90 instead of $3.30, saving $0.40 × 2,000,000 = $800,000.

8. {#bk2} [calc] A wheat farmer sells 20,000 bushels forward at $7.00. At harvest wheat trades at $6.20. How much more does the farmer receive than the market price?
   - [x] $16,000
   - $1,600
   - $140,000
   - $124,000
   > The farmer receives $7.00 instead of $6.20, a gain of $0.80 × 20,000 = $16,000.
