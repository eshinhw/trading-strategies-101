# Quiz

1. {#q1} What secures a pawn loan?
   - [x] A personal item the borrower hands over, held by the pawnbroker until the loan is repaid
   - The borrower's credit score
   - A co-signer's guarantee
   - Pawn loans require no collateral of any kind
   > The item itself is the collateral — held by the pawnbroker for the redemption period and forfeited if the loan isn't repaid.

2. {#q2} Why doesn't a pawnbroker need to check a borrower's credit history?
   - [x] The loan is fully secured by the item itself, capping the pawnbroker's risk regardless of the borrower's creditworthiness
   - Pawnbrokers are legally forbidden from checking credit
   - All pawnshop customers are pre-screened by banks
   - Pawn loans are always government-guaranteed
   > Since the collateral itself covers the pawnbroker's downside, there's no need to evaluate the borrower's ability or willingness to repay the way an unsecured lender would.

3. {#q3} What happens if a pawn loan isn't repaid within the redemption period?
   - [x] The pawnbroker keeps and resells the item, and the matter is fully settled
   - The borrower is sent to debt collection
   - The borrower's credit score is permanently damaged
   - The loan automatically renews forever at no cost
   > Forfeiture of the collateral is the pawnbroker's full recourse — there's no further debt collection process or credit consequence for the borrower.

4. {#q4} Why do pawn loan interest rates run higher than a typical bank loan?
   - [x] Small loan size, short duration, appraisal and storage costs, and the risk resale value won't fully cover the loan
   - Pawnshops are required to charge the maximum rate allowed nationwide
   - Pawn loans always have lower rates than bank loans
   - There is no cost basis for the higher rate — it's arbitrary
   > The higher rate reflects real costs and risks specific to small, short-term, appraisal-dependent lending, not an arbitrary markup.

5. {#q5} Who primarily uses pawn loans?
   - [x] Borrowers without easy access to traditional credit who need a small amount of cash quickly
   - Only borrowers with excellent credit scores
   - Large corporations financing inventory purchases
   - Pawn loans are exclusively used by licensed banks
   > Pawnbroking fills a gap for borrowers underserved by mainstream banking, offering fast, no-credit-check access to small amounts of cash.

6. {#calc1} [calc] A pawnbroker lends $300 at 4% a month (simple interest) for 3 months. How much must the borrower repay to get the item back?
   - $312
   - [x] $336
   - $348
   - $300
   > Interest is $300 × 4% × 3 = $36, so the repayment is $300 + $36 = $336.

7. {#calc2} [calc] A borrower defaults on a $270 pawn loan, and the pawnbroker resells the item, appraised at $900, for 70% of the appraisal. How much more than the loan does the pawnbroker receive?
   - $630
   - $270
   - $900
   - [x] $360
   > The item sells for 0.70 × $900 = $630, which is $630 − $270 = $360 more than was lent.

8. {#bk1} [calc] A pawnbroker lends $200 against a watch and charges 15% a month in simple interest. How much must the borrower repay to redeem it after 2 months?
   - $230
   - $266
   - $300
   - [x] $260
   > Interest is $200 × 15% × 2 = $60, so the borrower repays $200 + $60 = $260.

9. {#bk2} [calc] A pawnshop lends $150 on an item it values at $400. If the loan is not repaid and the shop sells the item for $400, what is its gross profit over the loan?
   - $150
   - [x] $250
   - $400
   - $550
   > The shop recovers $400 and lent $150, for a $250 gross profit before interest and costs.
