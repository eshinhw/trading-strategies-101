# Quiz

1. {#q1} What is withholding tax?
   - [x] A portion of a cross-border payment collected at the source and remitted to the source country's tax authority before the investor receives it
   - A tax assessed only after an investor files their annual tax return
   - A tax that applies only to domestic, not foreign, investors
   - Withholding tax has no connection to cross-border payments
   > Withholding tax is collected directly at the point of payment, distinguishing it from a tax assessed later through a filed return.

2. {#q2} Why can withholding rates differ depending on which two countries are involved?
   - [x] Tax treaties are negotiated bilaterally, so the effective rate depends on the specific country pair
   - Withholding rates are always identical worldwide with no variation
   - Withholding rates depend only on the size of the payment, never the countries involved
   - Tax treaties have no effect on withholding rates
   > Because treaty terms are negotiated between specific pairs of countries, the resulting withholding rate varies depending on which two countries are involved.

3. {#q3} What is a primary purpose of tax treaties?
   - [x] To prevent the same income from being taxed twice, once at the source and again in the investor's home country
   - To guarantee that every cross-border payment is taxed at 0%
   - Tax treaties exist solely to increase withholding rates
   - Tax treaties have no connection to double taxation
   > Avoiding double taxation on the same income is the core rationale behind tax treaties, commonly achieved by capping source-country withholding.

4. {#q4} Why does investor residency matter so much for withholding tax outcomes?
   - [x] Treaty benefits depend specifically on where an investor is resident, not on other characteristics of the investment
   - Investor residency has no bearing on withholding tax rates
   - Only the investment's currency determines the withholding rate
   - All investors, regardless of residency, always face an identical withholding rate
   > Because treaty eligibility is tied to residency, two investors holding the exact same security can face different withholding purely based on where they're tax resident.

5. {#q5} Two investors in different countries receive the same dividend from the same foreign company but face different withholding rates. Why?
   - [x] One investor's country of residence has a tax treaty with a reduced rate, while the other's does not
   - The two investors hold different securities, not the same dividend
   - Withholding rates are set randomly with no connection to residency or treaties
   - The company paying the dividend charges each investor a different price
   > The residency-driven treaty difference is exactly what produces the two different withholding outcomes on the same underlying payment.

6. {#calc1} [calc] Two investors each receive a $20,000 dividend. One is taxed at the 30% default rate and the other at a 15% treaty rate. How much more does the second investor receive?
   - $6,000
   - [x] $3,000
   - $4,000
   - $300
   > The first keeps $20,000 × 0.70 = $14,000 and the second keeps $20,000 × 0.85 = $17,000, a difference of $3,000.

7. {#calc2} [calc] A $40,000 dividend has 25% withheld at the source. How much does the investor receive?
   - $10,000
   - $37,500
   - $15,000
   - [x] $30,000
   > 25% × $40,000 = $10,000 is withheld, leaving $30,000.

8. {#bk1} [calc] A foreign company pays an investor a $10,000 dividend with 30% withholding tax. What does the investor receive? What if a treaty lowers the rate to 15%?
   - $7,000, or $9,000 under the treaty
   - $3,000, or $1,500 under the treaty
   - [x] $7,000, or $8,500 under the treaty
   - $7,000, or $7,500 under the treaty
   > At 30% withholding the investor receives $10,000 × 0.70 = $7,000. At 15% they receive $10,000 × 0.85 = $8,500.

9. {#bk2} [calc] An investor earns $10,000 of foreign dividends taxed at 15% abroad ($1,500) and at 24% at home. With a foreign tax credit, how much additional home tax is owed?
   - [x] $900
   - $2,400
   - $1,500
   - $3,900
   > Home tax is 24% × $10,000 = $2,400. The foreign tax credit removes $1,500, leaving $900 owed.

10. {#bk3} [calc] A foreign stock yields a 4% dividend and 15% is withheld at source. What is the net dividend yield?
   - 0.6%
   - 4.6%
   - [x] 3.4%
   - 3.85%
   > Net yield = 4% × (1 − 0.15) = 3.4%.
