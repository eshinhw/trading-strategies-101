# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A convertible has a bond floor of $920 and a conversion value of $800. Roughly what is the least it should trade for?
   - $800
   - $1,000
   - [x] $920
   - $1,720
   > Investors can hold the bond for its $920 floor value instead of converting into $800 of stock, so the price should not fall much below $920.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A convertible bond has a bond floor of $920 and a conversion value of $1,150. What is the minimum value it should have?
   - [x] $1,150
   - $920
   - $2,070
   - $1,035
   > A convertible is worth at least the greater of its bond floor and its conversion value, so $1,150.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A convertible has a conversion ratio of 20 and the option delta is 0.6. If the stock rises $5, by roughly how much does the bond price rise?
   - $100
   - $5
   - [x] $60
   - $30
   > Equivalent shares = 20 × 0.6 = 12. 12 × $5 = $60.

4. {#bk4} [calc] A convertible's bond floor is $920. Interest rates rise and the floor falls 4%. What is the new bond floor?
   - $916
   - [x] $883.20
   - $880
   - $957
   > $920 × (1 − 0.04) = $883.20.

5. {#bk5} [calc] A convertible trades at $1,150 with a bond floor of $920. How much is the market paying for the conversion option?
   - $1,150
   - $920
   - $2,070
   - [x] $230
   > Option value ≈ price − bond floor = $1,150 − $920 = $230.
