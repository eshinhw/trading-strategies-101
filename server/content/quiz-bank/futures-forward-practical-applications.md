# Quiz

1. {#q1} What single motivation underlies the common practical uses of forward contracts?
   - [x] Removing the uncertainty of a future transaction's price by locking in a fixed price today
   - Guaranteeing a profit regardless of market conditions
   - Avoiding all forms of financial risk entirely
   - Eliminating the need for any future transaction at all
   > Whether it's currency, commodity, or interest-rate exposure, each use traces back to trading away price uncertainty for a known, fixed number.

2. {#q2} How does an importer use a forward to hedge a foreign-currency payment?
   - [x] By buying the foreign currency forward today at a fixed rate, locking in how many dollars the future payment will cost
   - By waiting until the payment is due and hoping for a favorable rate
   - By selling the foreign currency forward instead of buying it
   - Importers cannot use forwards to hedge currency exposure
   > Buying the currency forward locks in today's exchange rate for a future payment obligation, removing the risk that the currency strengthens before the payment is due.

3. {#q3} How does an exporter's use of a currency forward differ from an importer's?
   - [x] The exporter sells the foreign currency forward to lock in future revenue, while the importer buys it forward to lock in a future payment
   - They are identical in every respect
   - Only importers can use forward contracts
   - Exporters can only use futures, never forwards
   > An exporter expecting to receive a foreign currency sells it forward to lock in its dollar value, the mirror image of an importer buying forward to lock in a payment's cost.

4. {#q4} How does a commodity producer use a forward contract to fix a future sale price?
   - [x] By going short a forward, agreeing today to sell future output at a fixed price
   - By going long a forward on their own product
   - Producers cannot hedge with forward contracts
   - By buying futures instead of forwards, since forwards don't apply to commodities
   > A producer's short forward position locks in a sale price for output that hasn't been produced or sold yet, the mirror image of a buyer such as a coffee roaster going long a forward.

5. {#q5} Which forward-style tool lets a company lock in a future borrowing rate?
   - [x] A forward rate agreement (FRA)
   - A commodity forward
   - An equity forward
   - There is no forward-based tool for interest-rate exposure
   > An FRA is the interest-rate equivalent of locking in a price — it lets a company fix today the rate that will apply to a future borrowing period.

6. {#q6} Why do forwards remain common in commodity supply agreements even where a liquid futures market exists for the same commodity?
   - [x] Because forwards let two commercial counterparties build exactly the deal their relationship needs — quantity, date, and location a standardized future can't match
   - Because forwards are always cheaper to trade than futures
   - Because futures contracts cannot be used for commodities
   - Because regulators require commercial parties to use forwards instead of futures
   > A forward's customizability is exactly what a standardized, one-size-fits-all futures contract can't offer — which is why both instruments coexist for the same underlying commodities.

7. {#calc1} [calc] An importer owes ¥60 million in three months and locks in ¥150 per dollar. At maturity the spot rate is ¥140 per dollar. How much does the forward save, in dollars?
   - About $400,000
   - [x] About $28,571
   - About $10,000
   - About $42,857
   > Hedged cost is ¥60M / 150 = $400,000. Unhedged it would be ¥60M / 140 ≈ $428,571, so the forward saves about $28,571.

8. {#bk1} [calc] An airline buys 2 million gallons of fuel forward at $2.90 per gallon. At delivery spot is $3.30. What is the value of the hedge?
   - $80,000
   - [x] $800,000
   - $1,600,000
   - $8,000,000
   > The airline pays $0.40 less per gallon: $0.40 × 2,000,000 = $800,000.

9. {#bk2} [calc] A company will receive €5 million in 3 months and sells euros forward at 1.0900. At delivery spot is 1.0600. How much more does the hedge deliver than selling at spot?
   - $15,000
   - $1,500,000
   - $300,000
   - [x] $150,000
   > (1.0900 − 1.0600) × €5,000,000 = $150,000.

10. {#bk3} [calc] An importer owes €2,000,000 and buys euros forward at 1.0950. At maturity spot is 1.0700. How much more does the hedge cost than buying at spot?
   - $5,000
   - [x] $50,000
   - $500,000
   - $2,190,000
   > The forward costs 1.0950 − 1.0700 = $0.0250 more per euro: $0.0250 × 2,000,000 = $50,000.

11. {#bk4} [calc] A company locks in a 6-month borrowing rate of 4.50% on $10 million with an FRA. The market rate turns out to be 5.20%. How much interest does the FRA save over the 6 months (before discounting)?
   - $70,000
   - $350,000
   - [x] $35,000
   - $3,500
   > The rate saved is 0.70%. $10,000,000 × 0.70% × 0.5 = $35,000.

12. {#bk5} [calc] A producer sells 100,000 gallons of heating oil forward at $2.80. At delivery spot is $2.55. What is the gain versus selling at spot?
   - [x] $25,000
   - $250,000
   - $2,500
   - $280,000
   > ($2.80 − $2.55) × 100,000 = $25,000.

13. {#bk6} [calc] A grower must hedge exactly 12,500 bushels. A standard contract is 5,000 bushels. If the grower uses 2 standard contracts, how many bushels stay unhedged?
   - 0
   - 5,000
   - [x] 2,500
   - 7,500
   > Two contracts hedge 10,000 bushels. 12,500 − 10,000 = 2,500 bushels stay unhedged. A custom forward could match 12,500 exactly.
