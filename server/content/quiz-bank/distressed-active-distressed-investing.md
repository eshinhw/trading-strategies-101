# Quiz

1. {#q1} What additional source of edge does active distressed investing add beyond recovery-value analysis?
   - [x] Using influence over the restructuring process itself to push toward a better outcome
   - Active investing removes the need for any recovery-value analysis at all
   - There is no additional source of edge in an active approach
   - Active investing relies entirely on random chance
   > Active distressed investing layers process influence on top of the same underlying recovery-value analysis a passive investor relies on.

2. {#q2} What does a seat on a creditors' committee provide?
   - [x] Direct visibility into the company's financial position and a formal voice in shaping the reorganization plan
   - Automatic full repayment of the investor's entire claim
   - No practical benefit over a purely passive position
   - Ownership of the company's physical assets immediately
   > A committee seat is a formal mechanism for influence, well beyond the limited visibility a passive bondholder has into the process.

3. {#q3} How can a large position size create negotiating leverage in a reorganization?
   - [x] It can effectively block a plan vote in that class, since plans typically need a threshold share of approval
   - Position size has no bearing on negotiating leverage
   - Large positions automatically forfeit all voting rights
   - Only equity holders, never debtholders, have any voting leverage
   > The voting-threshold structure of plan approval is exactly what turns a large enough position into real negotiating power.

4. {#q4} What is a real cost of pursuing an active distressed-investing approach?
   - [x] Legal and advisory resources, time, and trading restrictions tied to holding material non-public information
   - Active investing has no costs of any kind compared to a passive approach
   - Active investors are legally guaranteed a full recovery regardless of outcome
   - Active involvement always shortens the restructuring timeline to zero
   > The added influence of an active approach comes with real resource costs and constraints that a passive strategy doesn't face.

5. {#q5} A distressed investor joins the creditors' committee when the company's first proposed plan pays bondholders mostly in low-value warrants. What can that seat achieve that a passive bondholder couldn't?
   - [x] Negotiate a larger equity stake instead of accepting a plan paying mostly low-value warrants
   - An automatic doubling of the original investment with no negotiation required
   - Force immediate liquidation of the entire company
   - Nothing — active and passive investors always end up with the same outcome
   > The committee seat gave the investor real influence to negotiate a materially better outcome than simply accepting whatever plan was initially proposed.

6. {#calc1} [calc] An investor owns 12% of a $150 million unsecured bond class, bought at 40 cents. The negotiated plan pays the class 45 cents. What is the investor's profit?
   - [x] $0.9 million
   - $9.0 million
   - $0.45 million
   - $8.1 million
   > Face value held is 12% × $150M = $18M. Cost is $18M × 0.40 = $7.2M, and recovery is $18M × 0.45 = $8.1M, so the profit is $0.9M.

7. {#calc2} [calc] A reorganized company's equity is worth $120 million. An $80 million unsecured class receives 25% of it. How many cents on the dollar does the class recover?
   - 25 cents
   - 66.7 cents
   - [x] 37.5 cents
   - 150 cents
   > The class gets 25% × $120M = $30M, and $30M / $80M = 37.5 cents on the dollar.

8. {#bk1} [calc] A plan needs approval by at least two-thirds in amount of a class holding $240 million of claims. How much must an investor hold to be able to block it?
   - [x] More than $80 million
   - More than $120 million
   - More than $160 million
   - $240 million
   > Blocking needs more than one-third: $240 million / 3 = $80 million, so just over $80 million.

9. {#bk2} [calc] An investor buys $80 million of face value of a class at 45 cents to gain a blocking position. What does the stake cost?
   - $80 million
   - $44 million
   - [x] $36 million
   - $3.6 million
   > Cost = $80 million × 0.45 = $36 million.
