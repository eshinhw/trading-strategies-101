# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] An investor wants exposure to 100 stocks. Buying each stock costs a $5 commission, while one ETF trade costs $5. How much commission does the ETF save?
   - $500
   - $5
   - $95
   - [x] $495
   > Buying 100 stocks costs 100 × $5 = $500 versus $5 for one ETF trade, saving $495.

2. {#bk2} [calc] An investor holds an ETF with 50 equally weighted stocks, and one of them falls 40%. What is the approximate loss to the ETF?
   - 40%
   - [x] 0.8%
   - 8%
   - 2%
   > Each stock is 2% of the fund. 2% × 40% = 0.8%.

3. {#bk3} [calc] An investor puts $50,000 in an ETF with an expense ratio of 0.05% rather than a fund charging 0.80%. How much a year does the lower fee save?
   - $37.50
   - $3,750
   - $400
   - [x] $375
   > The fee gap is 0.75%. 0.75% × $50,000 = $375.

4. {#bk4} [calc] $100,000 earns 7% a year before fees for 10 years. How much more is it worth with a 0.05% fee than with a 0.80% fee (subtract each fee from the yearly return)?
   - [x] $13,305
   - $7,500
   - $17,000
   - $75,000
   > At 6.95% it grows to $195,798. At 6.20% it grows to $182,493. The difference is $13,305.

5. {#bk5} [calc] A mutual fund passes a $2,400 capital gain distribution to an investor, taxed at 15%. A similar ETF avoids the distribution through in-kind redemptions. How much tax does the ETF save this year?
   - $2,400
   - $2,040
   - [x] $360
   - $36
   > Tax = 15% × $2,400 = $360 that the ETF avoids.

6. {#bk6} [calc] An investor wants exposure to 10 stocks and pays a $5 commission per trade. One ETF holding all 10 costs a single $5 commission. How much does the ETF save?
   - [x] $45
   - $50
   - $5
   - $40
   > Buying 10 stocks costs $50. One ETF trade costs $5, saving $45.

7. {#bk7} [calc] A semiconductor ETF is 20% of a $100,000 portfolio and returns +12%. What is its contribution to the portfolio's dollar return?
   - $12,000
   - $20,000
   - [x] $2,400
   - $240
   > 20% × $100,000 = $20,000 invested. $20,000 × 12% = $2,400.

8. {#bk8} How does buying one ETF share help reduce single-company risk?
   - It removes all market risk
   - It guarantees every holding rises
   - It concentrates the position in one company
   - [x] It gives exposure to many underlying securities at once, so one holding's failure matters little
   > The ETF spreads the investment across dozens, hundreds or thousands of securities.

9. {#bk9} Why are most ETFs cheaper than a comparable actively managed mutual fund?
   - They are subsidized by the government
   - [x] Most are passively managed, tracking an index without paying a team to pick stocks
   - They hold no securities
   - They never trade
   > Passive tracking avoids the research cost of active management, so expense ratios are typically far lower.

10. {#bk10} Why can most ETFs avoid passing capital gains to their shareholders?
   - [x] In-kind creation and redemption trades baskets of securities instead of cash, so the fund does not have to sell holdings
   - ETFs are exempt from all taxes
   - ETFs never hold securities that gain value
   - ETFs pay out all gains as dividends
   > The in-kind mechanism lets an ETF hand over low-cost shares to redeeming participants instead of selling them.
