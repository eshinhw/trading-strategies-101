# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A stock trades at $40 with earnings of $6 per share and book value of $50 per share. What is its earnings yield?
   - 6.7%
   - [x] 15%
   - 125%
   - 30%
   > Earnings yield = earnings / price = $6 / $40 = 15.0%.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A stock trades at $40 with book value of $50 per share. What is its price-to-book ratio?
   - 1.25
   - 8.00
   - 0.15
   - [x] 0.80
   > Price-to-book = $40 / $50 = 0.80.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A stock trades at $75 with earnings of $3 per share and book value of $60 per share. What is its earnings yield?
   - 25%
   - [x] 4%
   - 80%
   - 8%
   > Earnings yield = earnings / price = $3 / $75 = 4.0%.

4. {#bk4} [calc] A stock trades at $75 with book value of $60 per share. What is its price-to-book ratio?
   - 0.80
   - 12.50
   - [x] 1.25
   - 0.04
   > Price-to-book = $75 / $60 = 1.25.

5. {#bk5} [calc] A value strategy ranks stocks on two cheapness measures. Stock A ranks 2nd on earnings yield and 6th on book-to-price. Stock B ranks 4th and 3rd. The strategy averages the ranks (lower is cheaper). Which stock is cheaper, and what are the averages?
   - [x] Stock B, average 3.5 against 4.0
   - Stock A, average 4.0 against 3.5
   - Stock A, average 2.0 against 3.0
   - Stock B, average 7.0 against 8.0
   > Stock A averages (2 + 6) / 2 = 4.0. Stock B averages (4 + 3) / 2 = 3.5. The lower average is cheaper, so Stock B.

6. {#bk6} [calc] A stock has a P/E of 8 while its sector averages 16. By what fraction is its P/E below the sector average?
   - 8
   - 100%
   - [x] 50%
   - 200%
   > (16 − 8) / 16 = 50% cheaper on this measure.

7. {#bk7} [calc] A value strategy buys the cheapest 20% of 500 stocks on price-to-book. How many stocks does it buy, and how large is each position if it invests $1 million equally?
   - [x] 100 stocks at $10,000 each
   - 20 stocks at $50,000 each
   - 100 stocks at $1,000 each
   - 500 stocks at $2,000 each
   > 20% × 500 = 100 stocks. $1,000,000 / 100 = $10,000 each.

8. {#bk8} What is a value trap?
   - A stock that is expensive but keeps rising
   - [x] A stock that looks cheap on a metric but is cheap for a real reason, such as a declining business
   - A stock that pays no dividends
   - A stock that trades only on small exchanges
   > Cheap stocks are often cheap because of weaker growth or higher risk, so the value premium is partly compensation for that risk.

9. {#bk9} Why is combining value and momentum attractive?
   - They are the same signal described in two ways
   - Both always do well in the same years
   - Combining them removes all risk
   - [x] They have historically shown low or negative correlation, so each tends to do well when the other struggles
   > A cheap stock is often a recent laggard, which momentum dislikes. This diversifies the combined portfolio.
