# Quiz

1. {#q1} What does novation mean in the context of a futures clearinghouse?
   - [x] The original bilateral trade between buyer and seller is replaced by two new contracts, each with the clearinghouse as counterparty
   - The trade is simply cancelled after being matched
   - The buyer and seller must renegotiate the price directly
   - Novation only applies to forward contracts, never futures
   > Novation is the legal substitution that inserts the clearinghouse as the counterparty to both sides, replacing the original buyer-seller contract with two new ones.

2. {#q2} Why doesn't a futures trader need to assess the creditworthiness of the trader on the other side of their trade?
   - [x] Because the clearinghouse becomes the counterparty to both sides through novation, not the original trader
   - Because all futures traders are required to be the same size
   - Because futures trades are never actually matched with anyone
   - Because creditworthiness is irrelevant to any financial contract
   > Since novation makes the clearinghouse the counterparty to every trade, a trader's actual exposure is to the clearinghouse itself, not to whichever specific trader happened to take the other side.

3. {#q3} How does the clearinghouse limit its own exposure to any single position?
   - [x] Through daily margining — initial margin, mark-to-market, and margin calls
   - By refusing to guarantee any trades at all
   - By requiring physical delivery on every contract the same day it's opened
   - The clearinghouse has no mechanism to limit its exposure
   > Daily mark-to-market and margin calls mean losses are collected in cash each day, so the clearinghouse is rarely exposed to more than a single day's adverse move on any position.

4. {#q4} What is a clearinghouse's guarantee (or default) fund for?
   - [x] To absorb losses if a member defaults and its own posted margin isn't enough to cover the loss
   - To pay dividends to clearing members
   - To replace the need for any margin requirements at all
   - To fund the exchange's marketing budget
   > The mutualized guarantee fund is a backstop beyond individual margin — a final layer of protection for the rare case where a defaulting member's own margin falls short.

5. {#q5} How does novation change a trader's counterparty risk compared to a private forward contract?
   - [x] It replaces exposure to one specific, possibly unknown counterparty with exposure to a well-capitalized, guarantee-fund-backed clearinghouse
   - It has no effect on counterparty risk at all
   - It increases counterparty risk compared to a forward
   - It eliminates the need for a clearinghouse entirely
   > Novation is precisely what converts the bilateral counterparty risk of a forward into exposure to a single, well-margined, guarantee-fund-backed clearinghouse instead.

6. {#calc1} [calc] A defaulting member owes a $12,000 loss and posted $7,500 of margin. How much does the guarantee fund have to cover?
   - $7,500
   - $12,000
   - $19,500
   - [x] $4,500
   > The margin absorbs $7,500 first, leaving $12,000 − $7,500 = $4,500 for the guarantee fund.

7. {#bk1} [calc] After novation a trade of 100 contracts between buyer A and seller B is replaced by two contracts with the clearinghouse. What is the clearinghouse's net position?
   - Long 100 contracts
   - Short 100 contracts
   - Long 200 contracts
   - [x] Zero, because it is long 100 to B and short 100 to A
   > The clearinghouse becomes the seller to A and the buyer from B in equal size, so its net market exposure is zero.

8. {#bk2} [calc] A clearing member's customers hold 30 long and 20 short contracts of the same future. The clearinghouse charges $5,000 per contract on the net position. What is the margin?
   - $250,000
   - [x] $50,000
   - $150,000
   - $100,000
   > The net position is 30 − 20 = 10 contracts. 10 × $5,000 = $50,000.

9. {#bk3} [calc] A clearing member defaults with a $12 million loss. Its own margin of $5 million is used first, then the $20 million guarantee fund. How much is left in the guarantee fund?
   - $8 million
   - $15 million
   - $20 million
   - [x] $13 million
   > The member's margin covers $5 million. The remaining $7 million comes from the fund, leaving $20 million − $7 million = $13 million.

10. {#bk4} [calc] Crude oil falls $1.20 in a day. Traders who are long 10 contracts (1,000 barrels each) are settled by the clearinghouse. How much do those longs pay in total that day?
   - [x] $12,000
   - $1,200
   - $120,000
   - $10,000
   > Loss = $1.20 × 1,000 × 10 = $12,000, paid in cash through daily settlement.

11. {#bk5} What does novation do to a futures trade?
   - It cancels the trade and returns margin to both traders
   - It makes the buyer and seller guarantee each other's performance
   - [x] It replaces the original contract with two: one between the buyer and the clearinghouse, one between the clearinghouse and the seller
   - It converts the futures contract into a forward contract
   > After novation each trader's counterparty is the clearinghouse, so no one needs to assess the creditworthiness of whoever was on the other side.

12. {#bk6} How does the clearinghouse keep its exposure small?
   - [x] Daily mark-to-market and margin calls mean it is rarely exposed to more than about a day's adverse move
   - It lends its own money to traders who lose
   - It only accepts trades from members with perfect credit ratings
   - It limits every trader to one contract
   > Because gains and losses are collected in cash every day, a loss cannot build up unpaid for long.

13. {#bk7} What is the guarantee fund at a clearinghouse?
   - A fund paid for by the exchange to give traders profits
   - A fund the defaulting member alone contributes to after it fails
   - [x] A pool contributed to by all clearing members that absorbs losses when a defaulting member's margin is not enough
   - A fund used only to pay brokers' commissions
   > The mutualized fund is a backstop behind each member's own margin and the daily margining process.
