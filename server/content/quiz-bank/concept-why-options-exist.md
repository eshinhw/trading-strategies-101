# Quiz

1. {#q1} What choice do options add for an investor worried about a stock falling?
   - Selling the stock is the only way to avoid risk
   - [x] Paying a premium for protection that matters only if the stock falls, while keeping the shares
   - Borrowing more money to buy shares
   - Waiting for the stock to recover
   > An option can protect against a drop without requiring the investor to sell and give up potential gains.

2. {#q2} What is the most an option buyer can lose?
   - An unlimited amount
   - [x] The premium paid for the option
   - The full value of the stock
   - Twice the premium
   > The buyer has a right, not an obligation, so the loss is capped at the premium.

3. {#q3} Which of these is a typical reason to sell options?
   - To buy stock at any price
   - [x] To collect premium income in exchange for taking on an obligation
   - To avoid paying commissions
   - To guarantee a profit
   > Sellers receive premium and accept the obligation to buy or sell at the strike if the option is exercised.

4. {#q4} Why did listed options become much easier to trade after 1973?
   - They became free to trade
   - [x] Standardized contracts and a clearinghouse guarantee made them liquid and reliable
   - They were no longer risky
   - Only banks could trade them
   > The Chicago Board Options Exchange introduced standard terms and a central guarantee for every contract.

5. {#q5} Why can options express views that stocks alone cannot?
   - They always rise
   - [x] Their payoffs can be shaped to bet on direction, a range, or how much a stock moves
   - They have no expiration
   - They are exempt from market moves
   > Because of their asymmetry and variety of combinations, options can produce payoffs a plain stock position can't.

6. {#calc1} [calc] A manager holds 50,000 shares at $40 and buys puts on all of them with a strike of 38 for $1.20 each. The stock falls to $30. What is the net loss?
   - $500,000
   - $100,000
   - [x] $160,000
   - $60,000
   > The stock loses 50,000 × $10 = $500,000. The puts pay (38 − 30) × 50,000 = $400,000 and cost $60,000, so the net loss is $500,000 − $400,000 + $60,000 = $160,000.
