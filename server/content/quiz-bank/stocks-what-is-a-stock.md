# Quiz

1. {#q1} What does owning a share of stock represent?
   - A loan to the company that must be repaid on a fixed schedule
   - [x] A fractional ownership stake in the company
   - A guarantee of a fixed annual payment
   - A right to manage the company's daily operations directly
   > A share is ownership, not debt — a shareholder owns a proportional slice of the company itself, with no promise of repayment the way a bondholder has.

2. {#q2} What is a shareholder's claim on a company's assets if it's ever liquidated?
   - Shareholders are paid before any creditor or bondholder
   - [x] A residual claim, paid only after every creditor and bondholder has been paid first
   - Shareholders have no claim on the company's assets at all
   - Shareholders receive a fixed dollar amount regardless of what's left
   > Shareholders sit at the bottom of the claims hierarchy in a liquidation — they only receive whatever is left after creditors and bondholders have been paid in full.

3. {#q3} What distinguishes a public company from a private one?
   - [x] A public company's shares are listed on an exchange and can be freely bought or sold by anyone, while a private company's shares are held by a small, fixed group
   - Private companies are always larger than public companies
   - Public companies cannot ever pay dividends
   - There is no meaningful difference between the two
   > Going public via an IPO is what opens a company's shares to trading by the general public on an exchange — a private company's ownership stays concentrated among a small group.

4. {#q4} What fundamentally causes a stock's price to move?
   - The price is fixed permanently at the company's IPO price
   - [x] The market's collective, ever-changing view of the company's future profits, updated as new information arrives
   - Stock prices change randomly with no relationship to the company at all
   - Only the company's own management can change the stock price
   > A stock's price reflects what buyers and sellers currently believe the company's future profits are worth today — new information shifts that view, and the price moves with it.

5. {#q5} What does a shareholder receive that a bondholder does not?
   - A fixed, guaranteed repayment date
   - [x] A vote on major corporate matters at shareholder meetings
   - Priority over shareholders in a liquidation
   - A fixed coupon payment schedule
   > Voting rights on major corporate matters are a feature of equity ownership, not debt — a bondholder is owed fixed payments but generally has no vote in how the company is run.

6. {#calc1} [calc] A company with 16 million shares sells 4 million new shares at $25 in an IPO. How much does it raise, and what share of the company do the new buyers own?
   - [x] $100 million, and 20%
   - $100 million, and 25%
   - $400 million, and 20%
   - $100 million, and 80%
   > 4,000,000 × $25 = $100M raised. The new buyers own 4 / (16 + 4) = 20%.

7. {#bk1} [calc] An investor owns 500 shares of a company with 20 million shares outstanding. What percentage of the company does the investor own?
   - 0.025%
   - 0.25%
   - [x] 0.0025%
   - 2.5%
   > 500 / 20,000,000 = 0.000025, or 0.0025%.

8. {#bk2} [calc] A company earns $40 million and an investor owns 5% of its shares. What is the investor's proportional claim on those profits?
   - [x] $2 million
   - $200,000
   - $20 million
   - $8 million
   > 5% × $40 million = $2 million. The profit is the company's, but each share carries a proportional claim on it.

9. {#bk3} [calc] An investor buys 200 shares at $40 and the company fails, leaving the shares worthless. Because of limited liability, what is the most the investor can lose?
   - $40
   - $80,000
   - [x] $8,000
   - More than $8,000, because creditors can pursue the investor
   > The most a shareholder can lose is what was paid: 200 × $40 = $8,000.

10. {#bk4} [calc] A company has 10 million shares, each with one vote. A shareholder owns 50,000 shares. What share of the vote does the shareholder hold?
   - 5%
   - [x] 0.5%
   - 0.05%
   - 50%
   > 50,000 / 10,000,000 = 0.5%.

11. {#bk5} How does owning a share differ from holding a company's bond?
   - A share is a loan with a fixed repayment, while a bond is an ownership stake
   - Both are fixed repayments, but a share pays more
   - A share has no claim on profits, while a bond does
   - [x] A share is a fractional ownership stake with no fixed repayment, while a bond is a loan owed a fixed repayment
   > Shareholders own a proportional slice of what the company owns and earns, with no promised payment. Bondholders are creditors owed a fixed sum.

12. {#bk6} What is a stock's price at any moment?
   - The value set each day by the company's board
   - [x] Whatever the market is currently willing to pay for one share, reset by real buy and sell orders
   - The company's book value divided by shares outstanding
   - The average price of the last year
   > The price reflects the market's collective, ever-changing view of the company's future prospects.
