# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A Treasury future quote of 112'16 means 112 and 16/32. What is that as a decimal price?
   - [x] 112.50
   - 112.16
   - 112.32
   - 113.60
   > 16/32 = 0.50, so the price is 112.50, not 112.16.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A Treasury future is worth $1,000 per full point. It moves from 112'16 to 113'08. What is the change in value per contract?
   - +$8
   - +$240
   - [x] +$750
   - +$7,500
   > 112'16 is 112.50 and 113'08 is 113.25. The move is +0.75 points, or 0.75 × $1,000 = $750.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A Treasury future quotes 98'24. What is the decimal price?
   - [x] 98.75
   - 98.24
   - 98.80
   - 98.60
   > 24/32 = 0.75, so the price is 98.75.

4. {#bk4} [calc] An index future settled yesterday at 4,480 and trades at 4,515 today. What is the daily change in points and percent?
   - +35 points, +7.8%
   - +35 points, +0.08%
   - +35 points, +0.35%
   - [x] +35 points, +0.78%
   > The change is 4,515 − 4,480 = 35 points. As a percentage: 35 / 4,480 = 0.78%.

5. {#bk5} [calc] A crude oil contract (1,000 barrels) has a day's high of $82.40 and low of $80.90. What was the day's range in dollars per contract?
   - $1.50
   - [x] $1,500
   - $15,000
   - $150
   > The range is $82.40 − $80.90 = $1.50 per barrel. $1.50 × 1,000 = $1,500 per contract.

6. {#bk6} [calc] A quote line shows volume of 90,000 contracts and open interest up by 9,000. What share of the day's volume became net new open interest?
   - 1%
   - 90%
   - 100%
   - [x] 10%
   > 9,000 / 90,000 = 10%.

7. {#bk7} In a futures ticker such as ESM5, what does the letter M identify?
   - The exchange it trades on
   - [x] The expiration month, June
   - The contract multiplier
   - The month of the first trade, May
   > The root symbol (ES) names the underlying, the letter is the expiration month (M is June) and the digit is the year.

8. {#bk8} Why is a Treasury quote of 112'16 not the same as 112.16?
   - [x] The part after the apostrophe is in 32nds, so 112'16 means 112 and 16/32, or 112.50
   - It is in 100ths, so it equals 112.16
   - It is in 10ths, so it equals 112.160
   - The apostrophe marks a negative price
   > Treasury futures quote in fractions of a point, traditionally 32nds. 16/32 = 0.50.

9. {#bk9} How is a price move on unusually high volume generally read compared with the same move on low volume?
   - As less significant, because heavy volume means prices are unreliable
   - As identical, because volume never matters
   - [x] As more significant, because more trading activity stood behind it
   - As a sign that the contract will be delisted
   > A move backed by heavy trading shows more conviction than the same move on thin volume.
