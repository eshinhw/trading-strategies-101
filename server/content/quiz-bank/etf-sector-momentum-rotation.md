# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] Three sector ETFs returned +8%, +3%, and −2% over the past three months. A rotation strategy buys the top sector only and it earns +4% next month, while the others earn +1% and +2%. What is the strategy's return that month?
   - 2.33%
   - [x] 4%
   - 3%
   - 1%
   > The rule holds only the top-ranked sector (+8% last quarter), which then returns +4%, so the strategy returned 4%.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A rotation strategy splits capital equally across the top two of five sector ETFs. Next month those two earn +3% and +1%. What is the portfolio return?
   - 4%
   - 1.5%
   - 3%
   - [x] 2%
   > Equal weights give 0.5 × 3% + 0.5 × 1% = 2%.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A rotation strategy holds the top 2 of 6 sector ETFs by 3-month return, equally weighted. The last 3-month returns were Tech +9%, Energy +6%, Utilities +2%, and Health −1% (the others were lower). Next month Tech returns +1% and Energy returns −3%. What is the portfolio return?
   - +1%
   - [x] −1%
   - −2%
   - +2%
   > The top two are Tech and Energy. Equal weights give 0.5 × 1% + 0.5 × (−3%) = −1%.

4. {#bk4} [calc] Six-month sector returns are Tech +14%, Health +9%, Energy +3% and Utilities −2%. A rotation strategy holds the top two, equally weighted. Next month Tech returns +1.5% and Health +0.5%. What is the portfolio return?
   - +2.0%
   - +0.5%
   - [x] +1.0%
   - +1.5%
   > The top two are Tech and Health. 0.5 × 1.5% + 0.5 × 0.5% = 1.0%.

5. {#bk5} [calc] A rotation holds 3 sector ETFs equally weighted and replaces one each month. Each trade costs 0.05% per side. What is the monthly cost of selling one-third and buying one-third?
   - [x] 0.033%
   - 0.10%
   - 0.05%
   - 0.33%
   > One-third sold and one-third bought is 2/3 of the portfolio traded. 0.05% × 2/3 = 0.033%.

6. {#bk6} [calc] A sector rotation earns +2%, −1% and +3% in three consecutive months. What is the three-month compounded return?
   - 3.0%
   - 3.9%
   - [x] 4.0%
   - 6.0%
   > 1.02 × 0.99 × 1.03 = 1.0401, a 4.0% gain.

7. {#bk7} [calc] A sector ETF rose from $100 to $120 over six months. What is its six-month return?
   - [x] 20%
   - 16.7%
   - 120%
   - 2%
   > ($120 − $100) / $100 = 20%.

8. {#bk8} [calc] A rotation strategy returned 8.5% in a year while the market returned 6.0%. What is the excess return?
   - 14.5 percentage points
   - [x] 2.5 percentage points
   - 1.4 percentage points
   - 0.25 percentage points
   > 8.5% − 6.0% = 2.5 percentage points.

9. {#bk9} [calc] A rotation strategy holds one sector ETF. The sector then falls 9%. By how much does the portfolio fall, compared with a strategy holding the top three equally weighted if only one of them fell 9%?
   - −3% against −9%
   - −9% against −9%
   - −27% against −9%
   - [x] −9% against −3%
   > The single-sector strategy loses the full 9%. In the three-sector strategy that sector is only a third of the portfolio: 9% / 3 = 3%.

10. {#bk10} How does sector momentum rotation differ from single-stock momentum?
   - It ranks bonds instead of stocks
   - [x] It ranks entire sector ETFs by trailing performance, one level up from individual stocks
   - It holds every sector equally forever
   - It picks stocks inside only one sector
   > The idea is the same as stock momentum, applied to a handful of liquid sector ETFs that cover the whole market.

11. {#bk11} What extra risk does holding only one or a few sectors add compared with a broad momentum strategy?
   - No extra risk, since sectors are diversified
   - Lower trading costs only
   - Zero exposure to market risk
   - [x] Concentration risk, plus exposure to momentum crashes when leadership reverses
   > Few positions mean a single sector's bad month hurts more.
