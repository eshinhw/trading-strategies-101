# Quiz

1. {#q1} What does sector momentum rotation rank, instead of individual stocks?
   - Individual bonds
   - [x] Entire sector ETFs, by their trailing return
   - Currency pairs
   - Commodity futures contracts
   > Sector momentum rotation applies the momentum idea one level up from individual stocks, ranking and rotating among whole sector ETFs based on their recent performance.

2. {#q2} What is one practical advantage of doing momentum at the sector level rather than the individual-stock level?
   - It requires holding thousands of individual positions
   - [x] A small number of liquid sector ETFs can cover the entire market with far fewer trades and less single-stock idiosyncratic risk
   - It eliminates all risk from the strategy
   - It requires no rebalancing at all
   > A handful of sector ETFs gives broad market coverage with simpler, less frequent rebalancing than a stock-picking momentum strategy spanning hundreds of individual names.

3. {#q3} How does a sector momentum rotation strategy typically decide which sector(s) to hold?
   - By picking sectors at random each month
   - [x] By ranking sector ETFs on trailing return over a lookback window and holding the top-ranked one or few
   - By holding every sector equally at all times
   - By avoiding whichever sector has performed best recently
   > The strategy computes each sector's trailing return, ranks them, and concentrates the portfolio in the leading sector or sectors, rebalancing periodically as leadership shifts.

4. {#q4} Why does sector rotation carry more concentration risk than a broad-market momentum strategy?
   - It never actually holds any positions
   - [x] It's typically concentrated in just one or a few sectors at a time, rather than diversified across many individual names
   - Concentration risk does not apply to ETF-based strategies
   - Sector ETFs are risk-free by construction
   > Because the portfolio is typically weighted toward a small number of leading sectors rather than spread across the whole market, its fortunes are tied more tightly to how those specific sectors perform.

5. {#q5} What risk does sector momentum rotation share with single-stock price-momentum?
   - The risk of guaranteed, permanent outperformance
   - [x] The risk of a sharp, sudden reversal in leadership — a "momentum crash" — that can hurt the strategy right as it's fully committed to the prior leader
   - The risk of the ETF issuer changing its logo
   - There is no shared risk between the two approaches
   > Like single-stock momentum, sector rotation is vulnerable to abrupt shifts in relative leadership, which can occur suddenly around turning points in the economic cycle.

6. {#calc1} [calc] A rotation holds the top two sector ETFs equally with $100,000. Next month the sectors return +2% and −1%. What is the profit?
   - $1,000
   - [x] $500
   - $1,500
   - $50
   > Each ETF has $50,000: 2% × $50,000 = +$1,000 and −1% × $50,000 = −$500, so the profit is $500.

7. {#bk1} [calc] Three sector ETFs returned +8%, +3%, and −2% over the past three months. A rotation strategy buys the top sector only and it earns +4% next month, while the others earn +1% and +2%. What is the strategy's return that month?
   - 2.33%
   - [x] 4%
   - 3%
   - 1%
   > The rule holds only the top-ranked sector (+8% last quarter), which then returns +4%, so the strategy returned 4%.

8. {#bk2} [calc] A rotation strategy splits capital equally across the top two of five sector ETFs. Next month those two earn +3% and +1%. What is the portfolio return?
   - 4%
   - 1.5%
   - 3%
   - [x] 2%
   > Equal weights give 0.5 × 3% + 0.5 × 1% = 2%.

   - +1%

10. {#bk4} [calc] Six-month sector returns are Tech +14%, Health +9%, Energy +3% and Utilities −2%. A rotation strategy holds the top two, equally weighted. Next month Tech returns +1.5% and Health +0.5%. What is the portfolio return?

11. {#bk5} [calc] A rotation holds 3 sector ETFs equally weighted and replaces one each month. Each trade costs 0.05% per side. What is the monthly cost of selling one-third and buying one-third?

   > One-third sold and one-third bought is 2/3 of the portfolio traded. 0.05% × 2/3 = 0.033%.
