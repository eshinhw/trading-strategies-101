# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A trader builds a Bearish Short Seagull Spread: sells one $90 put, buys one $100 put, and sells one $110 call, for no net premium. What is the break-even stock price at expiration, per share?
   - [x] $100
   - $20
   - $10
   - $101
   > For this position (K1 = 90, K2 = 100, K3 = 110), the break-even stock price is $100 per share. Formula: S* = K2 + netCF (netCF>0) or K3 + netCF (netCF<0).

2. {#bk2} [calc] A trader builds a Bearish Short Seagull Spread: sells one $90 put, buys one $100 put, and sells one $110 call, for no net premium. What is the maximum profit on 8 contracts (100 shares each), in dollars?
   - $80
   - $1,000
   - [x] $8,000
   - $9,000
   > The maximum profit is $10 per share. One contract covers 100 shares, so 8 contracts give $10 × 100 × 8 = $8,000.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A trader builds a Bearish Short Seagull Spread: sells one $90 put, buys one $100 put, and sells one $110 call, for no net premium. The stock is at $78 at expiration. What is the profit or loss per share, counting the premium?
   - [x] +$10
   - −$10
   - +$11
   - +$9
   > At $78 the legs are worth +$10 per share before the premium, and the net premium adds $0, for +$10 per share (given K1 = 90, K2 = 100, K3 = 110).

5. {#bk5} [calc] A trader builds a Bearish Short Seagull Spread: sells one $90 put, buys one $100 put, and sells one $110 call, for no net premium. At expiration the stock is at $90. What is the total profit or loss on 6 contracts (100 shares each), in dollars?
   - +$60
   - [x] +$6,000
   - +$1,000
   - −$6,000
   > Per share the position makes +$10 at $90. For 6 contracts: +$10 × 100 × 6 = +$6,000.
