# Quiz

1. {#q1} How does buying a put help an investor who owns a stock?
   - It guarantees the stock will rise
   - [x] It sets a floor, since the put gains value if the stock falls
   - It eliminates all costs
   - It forces the stock to be sold
   > A protective put offsets losses on the stock, at the cost of the premium.

2. {#q2} What does an investor give up by selling a covered call?
   - The shares immediately
   - [x] Gains above the strike price, in exchange for premium income
   - All downside protection and the premium
   - The right to receive dividends forever
   > The seller collects premium but caps the upside at the strike.

3. {#q3} Why might a trader buy a call instead of buying the stock?
   - Options never expire
   - [x] The most that can be lost is the premium, while a stock position can lose more
   - Calls always cost more than stock
   - Calls pay dividends
   > A bought option has limited risk, although it has an expiration date.

4. {#q4} Which view could a long straddle express?
   - The stock will stay exactly flat
   - [x] The stock will move a lot, in either direction
   - The stock will only rise
   - The stock will pay a large dividend
   > A straddle profits from the size of the move, not its direction.

5. {#q5} What are the key questions when choosing a strategy?
   - Only the price of the option
   - [x] The view on direction, the size and speed of the expected move, and the acceptable risk
   - The color of the chart
   - Which broker is used
   > Direction, magnitude, timing, and risk tolerance determine which strategy fits.

6. {#calc1} [calc] An investor owns 100 shares at $100 and buys a 95 put for $2.00. The stock falls to $80. What is the net loss, counting the put's cost?
   - [x] $700
   - $2,000
   - $1,500
   - $1,300
   > The stock loses $2,000. The put pays (95 − 80) × 100 = $1,500 and cost $200, so the net loss is $2,000 − $1,500 + $200 = $700.
