# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A stock's channel runs from a lower bound of 40 to an upper bound of 50. The stock is at 47. How far up the channel is it?
   - 47%
   - 30%
   - 94%
   - [x] 70%
   > Position = (47 − 40) / (50 − 40) = 70%, near the upper bound where a channel trader would consider selling.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A channel trader buys at the lower bound of 40 and sells at the upper bound of 50. What is the return on a round trip?
   - 20%
   - [x] 25%
   - 10%
   - 50%
   > (50 − 40) / 40 = 25%.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A channel is built with a regression line at 45 and bands at plus or minus 2 standard errors, where the standard error is 2.5. What are the channel bounds?
   - 42.5 to 47.5
   - 35 to 55
   - 45 to 50
   - [x] 40 to 50
   > The bands are 45 ± 2 × 2.5 = 45 ± 5, which gives 40 and 50.

4. {#bk4} [calc] A stock oscillates within a channel with a lower bound of $48 and an upper bound of $56. A channel trader buys at $48.50 and sells at $55.50. What is the return?
   - [x] 14.4%
   - 12.5%
   - 16.7%
   - 7%
   > ($55.50 − $48.50) / $48.50 = 14.4%.

5. {#bk5} [calc] A stock breaks above the upper channel bound of $56 on twice its usual volume and closes at $57.50. How far above the bound is the close, as a percentage?
   - 1.5%
   - 5.2%
   - [x] 2.7%
   - 0.27%
   > ($57.50 − $56) / $56 = 2.7%.

6. {#bk6} How does a channel breakout differ from trading within the channel?
   - [x] A breakout bets that a decisive move outside a boundary, especially on high volume, starts a new trend
   - A breakout bets the price will return to the middle
   - A breakout buys at the lower bound only
   - A breakout means the channel is stronger
   > Within-channel trading fades the boundaries. A breakout trade follows the price when the range stops holding.
