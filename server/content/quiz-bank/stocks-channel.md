# Quiz

1. {#q1} What forms a price channel?
   - A single moving average line
   - [x] Two roughly parallel trendlines — an upper line connecting recent highs and a lower line connecting recent lows
   - The company's earnings report schedule
   - A single support level with no corresponding resistance
   > A channel is defined by a pair of parallel trendlines bounding the stock's recent trading range from above and below, unlike a single support or resistance level.

2. {#q2} What does a basic channel-trading strategy do when price approaches the lower boundary of the channel?
   - Sell or short the stock
   - [x] Buy the stock, expecting it to bounce back within the established range
   - Immediately close all positions in the stock
   - Increase the size of the channel
   > A basic channel strategy is a mean-reversion approach within the channel's structure — buying near the lower boundary on the expectation the price continues oscillating within the range.

3. {#q3} How can a channel be used to trade breakouts, rather than just range-bound oscillation?
   - Channels can never be used to trade breakouts
   - [x] A decisive move outside either boundary, especially on high volume, can signal a new trend starting, prompting the trader to reverse from range-trading to trading the breakout direction
   - Breakouts only occur inside the channel, never outside it
   - Channel breakouts are always ignored by traders
   > When price breaks decisively through a channel boundary, especially with strong volume, it's read as a signal the range no longer holds — a channel trader may switch from betting on reversion to betting on the new trend.

4. {#q4} Can a price channel itself be trending, rather than flat?
   - No, channels are always perfectly flat
   - [x] Yes — a channel can be flat, rising (uptrend channel), or falling (downtrend channel)
   - Channels can only exist in cryptocurrency markets
   - A channel becomes a support level once it starts trending
   > A channel is defined by the parallel trendlines connecting highs and lows, which can themselves slope upward, downward, or stay flat, capturing trending as well as sideways price action.

5. {#q5} Why do channel strategies typically pair the range-trading rule with a stop-loss or breakout plan?
   - Channels never fail once established
   - [x] A channel that has held reliably can still fail without warning, so relying on it holding indefinitely is risky without a plan for that scenario
   - Stop-losses are required by regulation for all channel trades
   - Channel boundaries are always perfectly objective and never subjective
   > Since channel boundaries are drawn somewhat subjectively and can break down unexpectedly, a purely range-trading approach without a breakout plan or stop-loss risks large losses if the range fails to hold.

6. {#calc1} [calc] A channel runs from $54 at the bottom to $62 at the top and the stock trades at $55. How far up the channel is it?
   - 10%
   - 87.5%
   - 1.8%
   - [x] 12.5%
   > The position is ($55 − $54) / ($62 − $54) = 1 / 8 = 12.5%, near the lower bound where a range trader would buy.

7. {#bk1} [calc] A stock's channel runs from a lower bound of 40 to an upper bound of 50. The stock is at 47. How far up the channel is it?
   - 47%
   - 30%
   - 94%
   - [x] 70%
   > Position = (47 − 40) / (50 − 40) = 70%, near the upper bound where a channel trader would consider selling.

8. {#bk2} [calc] A channel trader buys at the lower bound of 40 and sells at the upper bound of 50. What is the return on a round trip?
   - 20%
   - [x] 25%
   - 10%
   - 50%
   > (50 − 40) / 40 = 25%.

9. {#bk3} [calc] A channel is built with a regression line at 45 and bands at plus or minus 2 standard errors, where the standard error is 2.5. What are the channel bounds?
   - 42.5 to 47.5
   - 35 to 55
   - 45 to 50
   - [x] 40 to 50
   > The bands are 45 ± 2 × 2.5 = 45 ± 5, which gives 40 and 50.

10. {#bk4} [calc] A stock oscillates within a channel with a lower bound of $48 and an upper bound of $56. A channel trader buys at $48.50 and sells at $55.50. What is the return?
   - [x] 14.4%
   - 12.5%
   - 16.7%
   - 7%
   > ($55.50 − $48.50) / $48.50 = 14.4%.

11. {#bk5} [calc] A stock breaks above the upper channel bound of $56 on twice its usual volume and closes at $57.50. How far above the bound is the close, as a percentage?
   - 1.5%
   - 5.2%
   - [x] 2.7%
   - 0.27%
   > ($57.50 − $56) / $56 = 2.7%.

12. {#bk6} How does a channel breakout differ from trading within the channel?
   - [x] A breakout bets that a decisive move outside a boundary, especially on high volume, starts a new trend
   - A breakout bets the price will return to the middle
   - A breakout buys at the lower bound only
   - A breakout means the channel is stronger
   > Within-channel trading fades the boundaries. A breakout trade follows the price when the range stops holding.
