# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A company earns $3.20 per share and similar companies trade at 18 times earnings. What share price does that multiple imply?
   - $0.18
   - $21.20
   - [x] $57.60
   - $86.40
   > Price = earnings per share × P/E = $3.20 × 18 = $57.60.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A company earns $1.85 per share and similar companies trade at 22 times earnings. What share price does that multiple imply?
   - [x] $40.70
   - $0.08
   - $23.85
   - $61.05
   > Price = earnings per share × P/E = $1.85 × 22 = $40.70.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A company earns $5.40 per share and similar companies trade at 12 times earnings. What share price does that multiple imply?
   - $0.45
   - $17.40
   - [x] $64.80
   - $97.20
   > Price = earnings per share × P/E = $5.40 × 12 = $64.80.

4. {#bk4} [calc] A stock will pay a $2 dividend next year, dividends should grow 4% a year forever, and investors require a 9% return. What is the stock worth using the dividend growth model?
   - $22.22
   - [x] $40
   - $15.38
   - $60
   > Value = next dividend / (required return − growth) = $2 / (9% − 4%) = $40.

5. {#bk5} [calc] A stock will pay a $1.50 dividend next year, dividends should grow 5% a year forever, and investors require a 10% return. What is the stock worth using the dividend growth model?
   - $15
   - $10
   - $45
   - [x] $30
   > Value = next dividend / (required return − growth) = $1.50 / (10% − 5%) = $30.

6. {#bk6} [calc] A stock trades at $48 and earned $3.00 per share. What is its earnings yield?
   - 16%
   - [x] 6.25%
   - 3.00%
   - 12.5%
   > Earnings yield is earnings per share divided by price: $3.00 / $48 = 6.25%, the inverse of the 16× P/E.
