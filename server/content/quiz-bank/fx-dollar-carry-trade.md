# Quiz

1. {#q1} How does the dollar carry trade differ from ranking many individual currencies against each other?
   - [x] It treats the US dollar as one side of the trade against a broad basket of other currencies, based on the dollar's rate relative to the global average
   - It only ever trades two specific non-dollar currencies against each other
   - It has no relationship to interest rates at all
   - It is identical to a high-minus-low carry strategy in every respect
   > Rather than ranking many currencies individually, the dollar carry trade simplifies the structure to dollar-versus-basket, based on whether US rates sit above or below the global average.

2. {#q2} When does the dollar carry trade go long the dollar?
   - When US interest rates are relatively low compared to the global average
   - [x] When US interest rates are relatively high compared to the global average
   - The dollar carry trade never takes a long dollar position
   - Only when the dollar is the only currency being traded globally
   > The strategy goes long the dollar when US rates sit above the global average, collecting carry by holding the higher-yielding currency (the dollar) against the basket.

3. {#q3} Why does the dollar carry trade focus specifically on the dollar rather than treating it like any other currency?
   - The dollar has no special role in global markets
   - [x] The dollar's outsized role as the world's primary reserve and funding currency gives its interest-rate level broad, systematic effects on global capital flows
   - The dollar cannot be included in any basket-based strategy
   - US interest rates never change, unlike other countries' rates
   > Because of the dollar's central role in the global financial system, its rate relative to the rest of the world has systematic effects that a currency-by-currency ranking approach wouldn't directly capture.

4. {#q4} What primarily drives the dollar carry trade's return?
   - A completely different mechanism unrelated to any interest-rate differential
   - [x] The same basic carry logic as other carry trades — earning the rate differential — but concentrated into a single dollar-versus-basket position
   - Only the price of gold
   - The trade has no relationship to the Federal Reserve's policy
   > Like other carry trades, the return comes from the interest-rate differential, but here it's tied specifically to the dollar's rate relative to the global average rather than diversified across many pairs.

5. {#q5} Why can a short-dollar dollar carry position be particularly exposed to sudden reversals during risk-off episodes?
   - The dollar has no safe-haven characteristics
   - [x] The dollar often serves as a safe-haven currency, so capital can flow toward it during stress regardless of the interest-rate differential, causing it to strengthen sharply
   - Risk-off episodes have no effect on currency markets
   - A short-dollar position is always risk-free
   > Because the dollar tends to strengthen during global market stress as a safe haven, a position betting on dollar weakness can be hit hard by a sudden risk-off-driven dollar rally, independent of interest-rate differentials.

6. {#calc1} [calc] U.S. rates are 5.0% against a global average of 3.2%, so the rule goes long the dollar with $5 million. What is the annual carry?
   - [x] $90,000
   - $250,000
   - $160,000
   - $18,000
   > The differential is 1.8%, and 1.8% × $5,000,000 = $90,000.

7. {#bk1} [calc] A dollar carry rule goes long a basket of foreign currencies when the US interest rate is below the average foreign rate, and long the dollar otherwise. US rates are 5% and the average foreign rate is 3.5%. Which position does it take, and what is the yearly carry on $10 million?
   - Long foreign currencies, carry $150,000
   - [x] Long the dollar, carry $150,000
   - Long the dollar, carry $1.5 million
   - Long foreign currencies, carry $1.5 million
   > US rates are above the foreign average, so the rule is long the dollar. Carry = (5% − 3.5%) × $10 million = $150,000.

8. {#bk2} [calc] A trader is long the dollar against a foreign basket on $10 million, earning 1.5% in carry. The foreign basket appreciates 2% against the dollar. What is the net result?
   - +$350,000
   - −$200,000
   - +$150,000
   - [x] −$50,000
   > Carry = +$150,000. The currency loss = 2% × $10 million = $200,000. Net = −$50,000.

9. {#bk3} [calc] The US interest rate is 2% and the average foreign rate is 4.5%. A dollar carry rule goes long a foreign basket funded in dollars. What yearly carry does it earn on $5 million?
   - $75,000
   - [x] $125,000
   - $325,000
   - $12,500
   > Carry = (4.5% − 2%) × $5 million = 2.5% × $5 million = $125,000.
