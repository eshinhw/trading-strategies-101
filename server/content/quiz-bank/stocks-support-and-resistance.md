# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] Yesterday a stock had a high of 105, low of 95 and close of 100. What is the pivot point, and what are the first resistance and support levels (R1 = 2P − low, S1 = 2P − high)?
   - P = 100, R1 = 95, S1 = 105
   - P = 105, R1 = 110, S1 = 100
   - P = 100, R1 = 110, S1 = 90
   - [x] P = 100, R1 = 105, S1 = 95
   > P = (105 + 95 + 100) / 3 = 100. R1 = 2 × 100 − 95 = 105. S1 = 2 × 100 − 105 = 95.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A stock has a high of 52, low of 48 and close of 51. What is the pivot point?
   - 50.00
   - [x] 50.33
   - 51.00
   - 49.00
   > P = (52 + 48 + 51) / 3 = 50.33.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A trader buys a breakout at 106 above resistance of 105, places a stop at 103, and targets 112. What is the reward-to-risk ratio?
   - 1 to 2
   - 1 to 1
   - 3 to 1
   - [x] 2 to 1
   > Reward = 112 − 106 = 6. Risk = 106 − 103 = 3. The ratio is 6 / 3 = 2 to 1.

4. {#bk4} [calc] A stock bounces at support of $40 three times and a trader buys at $40.50 with a stop at $39. What is the maximum loss on 400 shares?
   - [x] $600
   - $400
   - $1,500
   - $60
   > Loss per share = $40.50 − $39 = $1.50. $1.50 × 400 = $600.

5. {#bk5} [calc] A stock fails at resistance of $75 and a trader shorts at $74.50, targeting $70 and stopping at $76. What is the reward-to-risk ratio?
   - 1 to 3
   - 4.5 to 1
   - [x] 3 to 1
   - 1 to 1
   > Reward = $74.50 − $70 = $4.50. Risk = $76 − $74.50 = $1.50. 4.50 / 1.50 = 3.

6. {#bk6} What is the main risk with support and resistance levels?
   - [x] They are subjective: different traders draw different levels, and a level can fade or fail
   - They always hold forever
   - They only work on bonds
   - They are set by the exchange
   > Because the levels are self-referential and drawn from a chart, a level's significance can disappear.
