# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] An ETF trades at a $0.04 bid-ask spread and a $80 share price. What is the spread as a percentage of price?
   - [x] 0.05%
   - 0.5%
   - 0.04%
   - 5%
   > $0.04 / $80 = 0.0005, or 0.05% (5 basis points).

2. {#bk2} [calc] An investor places a market order for 500 ETF shares quoted $30.00 bid / $30.06 ask. How much do they pay at the quoted ask?
   - $15,000
   - $15,015
   - [x] $15,030
   - $14,970
   > A market buy fills at the ask: 500 × $30.06 = $15,030.

3. {#bk3} [calc] An ETF quotes $99.98 bid and $100.02 ask. A trader buys 1,000 shares at the ask. How much is paid above the midpoint?
   - [x] $20
   - $40
   - $2
   - $200
   > The midpoint is $100.00. $0.02 × 1,000 = $20.

4. {#bk4} [calc] An ETF closes at $97.40 and its NAV is $98.00. What is the discount to NAV?
   - 0.6 cents
   - 6.1%
   - 0.06%
   - [x] 0.61%
   > ($98.00 − $97.40) / $98.00 = 0.61%.

5. {#bk5} [calc] An investor wants to invest $30,000 in an ETF with an ask of $50.00. How many whole shares can be bought?
   - 60
   - [x] 600
   - 6,000
   - 30
   > $30,000 / $50 = 600 shares.

6. {#bk6} [calc] A trader buys 500 ETF shares at the ask and sells at the bid, with a $0.06 spread. What does the round trip cost?
   - $3
   - $300
   - $0.06
   - [x] $30
   > $0.06 × 500 = $30.

7. {#bk7} [calc] An investor places a buy limit order at $100.00 when the ask is $100.02. Will it fill immediately?
   - Yes, at $100.02
   - [x] No, it fills only if the ask falls to $100.00 or lower
   - Yes, at $100.00
   - No, limit orders cannot be placed on ETFs
   > A buy limit order fills at the limit price or better. The ask is above it, so the order waits.

8. {#bk8} [calc] A 10 basis point round-trip spread cost is paid on an ETF held for 2 years. What is that cost per year?
   - [x] 0.05%
   - 0.10%
   - 0.2%
   - 0.50%
   > 0.10% / 2 = 0.05% a year.

9. {#bk9} What do ETFs use to place trades?
   - Special order types available only to institutions
   - Only market orders
   - [x] The same order types as stocks, such as market and limit orders
   - Only orders placed at the close
   > Because ETF shares trade on an exchange, the same order types and considerations apply.

10. {#bk10} What influences an ETF's bid-ask spread besides its own trading volume?
   - The color of the fund company's logo
   - [x] How liquid its underlying holdings are, since market makers can hedge using them
   - The investor's account size
   - The day of the week only
   > Market makers can lean on the underlying securities' liquidity, so ETFs of liquid holdings often trade with narrow spreads.

11. {#bk11} When can an ETF's market price differ most from its NAV?
   - Never, because the two are identical by law
   - Only at 4pm each day
   - Only when the ETF pays a dividend
   - [x] With less liquid ETFs or during volatile markets, although creation and redemption normally keep the gap small
   > A premium or discount can open briefly when trading conditions are stressed.
