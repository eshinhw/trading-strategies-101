# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] Corn futures are $5.40 and the local elevator pays the futures price less $0.25. What does a farmer receive for 40,000 bushels?
   - $216,000
   - $10,000
   - $5.15
   - [x] $206,000
   > The cash price is $5.40 − $0.25 = $5.15. $5.15 × 40,000 = $206,000.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A miller buys corn futures at $6.00 to hedge 10,000 bushels. At delivery the miller buys at the cash price of $6.50 and sells the futures at $6.45. What is the miller's net cost?
   - $65,000
   - [x] $60,500
   - $64,500
   - $60,000
   > The futures gain is $0.45 per bushel. Net cost per bushel = $6.50 − $0.45 = $6.05. $6.05 × 10,000 = $60,500.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A hedger sells 2 wheat futures (5,000 bushels each) at $6.20 and a speculator buys them. Wheat rises $0.10. What does the speculator gain, and what does the hedger lose on the futures?
   - $1,000 gain and $0 loss
   - $2,000 gain and $1,000 loss
   - $100 gain and $100 loss
   - [x] $1,000 gain and $1,000 loss
   > Each side's result is $0.10 × 10,000 = $1,000. Futures are zero-sum: the speculator gains what the hedger loses.

4. {#bk4} How does a farmer who never trades futures still benefit from the futures market?
   - [x] By using the public futures price to plan, for example when deciding how much to plant or what a buyer should pay
   - By receiving free insurance from the exchange
   - By being forced to deliver crops at the futures price
   - By avoiding all price risk automatically
   > Futures prices are a public, real-time estimate of future value, used far beyond the traders who generate them.

5. {#bk5} What does a hedger give up in exchange for locking in a price?
   - All of their exposure to the underlying business
   - The right to use the exchange again
   - [x] Some potential upside if the price moves in their favour
   - Any obligation to perform at the contract's expiry
   > Hedging trades uncertainty for certainty. The hedger gets a known price but misses out if the market moves their way.

6. {#bk6} Why is the last futures price considered a good consensus estimate of future value?
   - [x] It comes from continuous, transparent trading that reflects every buyer's and seller's information at once
   - The exchange sets it using a secret formula
   - It is the average of the last year's prices
   - It is whatever price a single large trader chooses
   > Open trading combines all participants' information and expectations into one public price.
