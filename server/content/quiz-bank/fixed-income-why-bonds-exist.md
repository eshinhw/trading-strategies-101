# Quiz

1. {#q1} Why might a borrower prefer issuing bonds to borrowing from a single bank?
   - Bonds are always cheaper than every other option
   - [x] It can reach many lenders at once, raise larger amounts, and lock in terms for longer
   - Bond lenders never need to be repaid
   - Banks cannot lend to governments
   > Splitting the loan across many investors lets a borrower raise more money, for longer, on more predictable terms.

2. {#q2} What does a bond do to a large loan?
   - It turns it into a stock
   - [x] It splits it into many small, standardized, tradable pieces
   - It cancels the borrower's obligation
   - It converts it into a gift
   > Each bond is a standardized piece of the larger loan with a face value, a rate, and a maturity, so many investors can lend and later trade it.

3. {#q3} Which of these is a typical reason a municipality issues bonds?
   - To pay shareholders a dividend
   - To buy shares of other companies
   - [x] To build public projects such as schools, bridges, or water systems
   - To fund an IPO
   > Municipal bonds are used to finance local public projects.

4. {#q4} How does issuing bonds differ from selling shares for a company?
   - Bonds give lenders a share of ownership
   - [x] Bonds don't give up ownership or future profits, since lenders are only owed what was promised
   - Shares must be repaid at maturity
   - There is no difference
   > A bondholder is a lender with a fixed claim, while a shareholder is an owner with a claim on profits.

5. {#q5} Why can bondholders sell their bonds before maturity?
   - Because the borrower always buys them back
   - Because bonds have no maturity
   - [x] Because the pieces are standardized and tradable, so another investor can buy them
   - Because the government requires it
   > Standardization lets bonds trade among investors, so a lender isn't locked in until the final repayment.

6. {#q6} [calc] The packaging manufacturer issues 50,000 bonds with a face value of $1,000 each. How much does it raise?
   - $5 million
   - $20 million
   - [x] $50 million
   - $500 million
   > 50,000 bonds × $1,000 of face value each is $50 million, which is $30 million more than the bank was willing to lend.

7. {#q7} [calc] The bonds pay a 5% fixed coupon. How much interest does the manufacturer pay each year, before tax?
   - $250,000
   - [x] $2,500,000
   - $5,000,000
   - $1,875,000
   > 5% of $50 million is $2.5 million a year. The $1,875,000 figure is the cost after a 25% tax deduction, not the amount paid.
