# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A trend-following rule is applied to 6 ETFs from different asset classes. 4 are in uptrends and the rule holds each uptrend at an equal 1/6 weight, with the rest in cash. How much of the portfolio is invested?
   - 100%
   - 33.3%
   - [x] 66.7%
   - 16.7%
   > 4 positions × 1/6 = 66.7% invested, with 33.3% in cash.

2. {#bk2} [calc] Four uncorrelated ETF trend positions each have a volatility of 12% and are equally weighted. What is the volatility of the portfolio?
   - [x] 6%
   - 12%
   - 3%
   - 48%
   > With uncorrelated equal weights, volatility falls with the square root of the number of positions: 12% / √4 = 6%.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A multi-asset trend rule sizes each ETF by target volatility divided by its own volatility. The target is 10% and an ETF has a volatility of 20%. What weight does it get?
   - 200%
   - 10%
   - [x] 50%
   - 20%
   > Weight = 10% / 20% = 50%.

4. {#bk4} [calc] A trend rule is applied to 8 ETFs with equal weights of 1/8 each. Five are in uptrends and are held long, and the other three are in cash. How much of the portfolio is invested?
   - 100%
   - [x] 62.5%
   - 37.5%
   - 5%
   > 5 × 1/8 = 62.5% is invested and 37.5% is in cash.

5. {#bk5} [calc] Three ETFs have volatilities of 5%, 10% and 20%. A portfolio weights them in proportion to the inverse of volatility. What weight goes to the 5% volatility ETF?
   - 33.3%
   - 71.4%
   - 14.3%
   - [x] 57.1%
   > Inverse volatilities are 20, 10 and 5, summing to 35. 20 / 35 = 57.1%.

6. {#bk6} [calc] A trend strategy targets 10% volatility per position. An ETF has a volatility of 25%. What weight does it get?
   - 250%
   - [x] 40%
   - 10%
   - 25%
   > Weight = 10% / 25% = 40%.

7. {#bk7} [calc] A trend portfolio holds four ETFs with equal 25% weights. They return +6%, +2%, −3% and 0%. What is the portfolio return?
   - 5%
   - 0.75%
   - 2%
   - [x] 1.25%
   > 0.25 × (6 + 2 − 3 + 0) = 0.25 × 5 = 1.25%.

8. {#bk8} Why apply one trend rule to ETFs across many asset classes?
   - Because all asset classes always trend together
   - To concentrate risk in equities
   - [x] Any one asset class may be trendless, but a broad basket of uncorrelated classes gives more chances for trends
   - Because it eliminates the need for a rule
   > Diversifying trend opportunity smooths returns and improves the odds that something is trending.

9. {#bk9} Why has multi-asset trend following tended to do well in equity crises?
   - [x] Downtrends are trends too, so the strategy can go short or move to cash during a selloff
   - It always holds more stocks in a crisis
   - It avoids all losses by design
   - It ignores asset prices entirely
   > A sustained fall is still a trend, and the rule can profit from it or step aside.

10. {#bk10} Why does a diversified multi-asset trend portfolio tend to have a smoother return profile?
   - Every asset class moves identically
   - [x] The underlying asset classes are relatively uncorrelated with each other
   - It holds only one asset
   - It does not use ETFs
