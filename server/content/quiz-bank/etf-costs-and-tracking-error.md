# Quiz

1. {#q1} How does an ETF's expense ratio reach the investor?
   - It's billed to the investor each year
   - [x] It's deducted gradually from the fund's assets, which lowers the investor's returns
   - It's paid only when the ETF is sold
   - It's paid to the exchange
   > The fee is taken from the fund's assets throughout the year, so the investor sees it as slightly lower returns rather than a bill.

2. {#q2} [calc] About how much does a 0.10% expense ratio cost per year on a 10,000 dollar investment?
   - 1 dollar
   - 100 dollars
   - [x] 10 dollars
   - 0.10 dollars
   > 0.10% of 10,000 dollars is 10 dollars a year.

3. {#q3} Which cost is paid at the time of each trade rather than every year?
   - The expense ratio
   - [x] The bid-ask spread
   - Tracking error
   - The index fee
   > Each trade happens at the bid or ask, so the spread is a cost paid when buying or selling.

4. {#q4} What does a low tracking error indicate?
   - The ETF has a low price
   - The ETF has low fees
   - [x] The ETF follows its index closely and consistently
   - The ETF is thinly traded
   > Tracking error measures how consistent the gap between the ETF and its index is, and a low value means the ETF stays close to its index.

5. {#q5} When is the expense ratio likely to matter more than the bid-ask spread?
   - Over a very short holding period
   - [x] Over a long holding period, since it is charged every year
   - Never, since the spread always dominates
   - Only for leveraged ETFs
   > The spread is paid per trade while the expense ratio accrues every year, so its effect grows the longer an ETF is held.

6. {#calc1} [calc] Fund A charges 0.04% and Fund B charges 0.25%. On $40,000 held for 10 years (ignore compounding), how much more does Fund B cost in fees?
   - $84
   - $100
   - $8,400
   - [x] $840
   > The fee gap is 0.21% a year, which is $40,000 × 0.21% = $84 a year, and $84 × 10 years = $840.

7. {#calc2} [calc] An index returns 9.85% and an ETF with a 0.20% expense ratio returns 9.62%. How much of the shortfall is unexplained by the fee?
   - 0.23%
   - [x] 0.03%
   - 0.20%
   - 0.17%
   > The shortfall is 9.85% − 9.62% = 0.23%, and the fee explains 0.20%, leaving 0.03% from trading and sampling.

   > Annual cost = $50,000 × 0.03% = $15.

11. {#bk4} [calc] An index returned 10.00% last year and the ETF tracking it returned 9.62%. What was the tracking difference?
   - +0.38 percentage points
   - [x] −0.38 percentage points
   - 3.80 percentage points
   - −0.19 percentage points
   > Tracking difference is the fund return minus the index return: 9.62% − 10.00% = −0.38 points.

   > Tracking difference is the fund return minus the index return: 14.70% − 15.00% = −0.30 points.

14. {#bk7} [calc] An investor buys $10,000 of an ETF with a 0.20% bid-ask half-spread when buying and 0.20% when selling, and a 0.10% annual expense ratio. After holding one year with the index flat, what is the total cost?
   - $10
   - $40
   - $30
   - [x] $50
   > Buying and selling each cost 0.20% = $20, for $40 in trading costs, plus 0.10% of $10,000 = $10 in fees, so $50 in total.

15. {#bk8} [calc] An investor holds $80,000 in an ETF with an expense ratio of 0.35%. What is the yearly fee?

   > 0.35% × $80,000 = $280.

16. {#bk9} [calc] $10,000 earns 7% a year before fees for 20 years. How much more is it worth with a 0.10% expense ratio than with a 0.60% expense ratio?

   - $3,000
   - $10,000
   > At 6.90% it grows to $37,980. At 6.40% it grows to $34,581. The difference is $3,399.

17. {#bk10} [calc] An index returned 12.00% and the ETF tracking it charges a 0.20% expense ratio and returned 11.70%. How much of the tracking difference is not explained by the fee?

   - 0.50 percentage points
   > Tracking difference = 11.70% − 12.00% = −0.30%. The fee explains −0.20%, leaving −0.10 points unexplained.

18. {#bk11} [calc] An investor pays a 0.20% expense ratio each year and 0.10% for the buy and sell spread once, holding for 5 years. What is the approximate total annual cost?

   - [x] 0.22%
   - 0.52%
   > The one-time 0.10% spread spread over 5 years is 0.02% a year. Adding the 0.20% fee gives 0.22%.

22. {#bk15} How is an ETF's expense ratio charged to the investor?
   - It is billed to the investor each month
   - It is charged once at purchase
   - It is paid only when the ETF is sold
   - [x] It is deducted gradually from the fund's assets, which shows up as slightly lower returns
   > Investors never see a bill for the fee. It is built into the fund's returns.

23. {#bk16} What is tracking difference?
   - The difference between bid and ask prices
   - [x] The gap between the ETF's return and its index's return over a period
   - The fund's yearly expense ratio
   - The difference between two ETFs' prices
   > Tracking difference is usually close to the expense ratio but is affected by other items such as holding a sample of securities.

24. {#bk17} Over a long holding period, which cost tends to matter most for an ETF?
   - The bid-ask spread, because it is paid every day
   - The commission on the first trade
   - Nothing, since costs vanish over time
   - [x] The expense ratio, since it is paid every year while the spread is paid when trading
   > Over a short period the spread can dominate. Over many years the yearly fee adds up.
