# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] An investor holds $50,000 in a low-cost index ETF with a 0.03% expense ratio. What is the annual fund cost?
   - $150
   - $1
   - [x] $15
   - $1,500
   > Annual cost = $50,000 × 0.03% = $15.

2. {#bk2} [calc] An investor holds $50,000 in a actively managed ETF with a 0.75% expense ratio. What is the annual fund cost?
   - [x] $375
   - $3,750
   - $38
   - $37,500
   > Annual cost = $50,000 × 0.75% = $375.

3. {#bk3} [calc] An investor holds $120,000 in a thematic ETF with a 0.45% expense ratio. What is the annual fund cost?
   - $5,400
   - $54
   - [x] $540
   - $54,000
   > Annual cost = $120,000 × 0.45% = $540.

4. {#bk4} [calc] An index returned 10.00% last year and the ETF tracking it returned 9.62%. What was the tracking difference?
   - +0.38 percentage points
   - [x] −0.38 percentage points
   - 3.80 percentage points
   - −0.19 percentage points
   > Tracking difference is the fund return minus the index return: 9.62% − 10.00% = −0.38 points.

5. {#bk5} [calc] An index returned 15.00% last year and the ETF tracking it returned 14.70%. What was the tracking difference?
   - +0.30 percentage points
   - 3.00 percentage points
   - −0.15 percentage points
   - [x] −0.30 percentage points
   > Tracking difference is the fund return minus the index return: 14.70% − 15.00% = −0.30 points.

6. {#bk6} [calc] An index returned −1.00% last year and the ETF tracking it returned −1.25%. What was the tracking difference?
   - +0.25 percentage points
   - [x] −0.25 percentage points
   - 2.50 percentage points
   - −0.12 percentage points
   > Tracking difference is the fund return minus the index return: −1.25% − (−1.00%) = −0.25 points.

7. {#bk7} [calc] An investor buys $10,000 of an ETF with a 0.20% bid-ask half-spread when buying and 0.20% when selling, and a 0.10% annual expense ratio. After holding one year with the index flat, what is the total cost?
   - $10
   - $40
   - $30
   - [x] $50
   > Buying and selling each cost 0.20% = $20, for $40 in trading costs, plus 0.10% of $10,000 = $10 in fees, so $50 in total.

8. {#bk8} [calc] An investor holds $80,000 in an ETF with an expense ratio of 0.35%. What is the yearly fee?
   - $2,800
   - $28
   - [x] $280
   - $350
   > 0.35% × $80,000 = $280.

9. {#bk9} [calc] $10,000 earns 7% a year before fees for 20 years. How much more is it worth with a 0.10% expense ratio than with a 0.60% expense ratio?
   - [x] $3,399
   - $500
   - $3,000
   - $10,000
   > At 6.90% it grows to $37,980. At 6.40% it grows to $34,581. The difference is $3,399.

10. {#bk10} [calc] An index returned 12.00% and the ETF tracking it charges a 0.20% expense ratio and returned 11.70%. How much of the tracking difference is not explained by the fee?
   - [x] 0.10 percentage points
   - 0.30 percentage points
   - 0.20 percentage points
   - 0.50 percentage points
   > Tracking difference = 11.70% − 12.00% = −0.30%. The fee explains −0.20%, leaving −0.10 points unexplained.

11. {#bk11} [calc] An investor pays a 0.20% expense ratio each year and 0.10% for the buy and sell spread once, holding for 5 years. What is the approximate total annual cost?
   - 0.30%
   - 0.20%
   - [x] 0.22%
   - 0.52%
   > The one-time 0.10% spread spread over 5 years is 0.02% a year. Adding the 0.20% fee gives 0.22%.

12. {#bk12} [calc] An investor buys $25,000 of an ETF with a 0.08% bid-ask spread cost on the trade. What is the spread cost?
   - [x] $20
   - $2
   - $200
   - $0.08
   > 0.08% × $25,000 = $20.

13. {#bk13} [calc] Two ETFs track the same index. One charges 0.03% and the other 0.45%. On a $250,000 holding, how much more does the second cost each year?
   - $1,125
   - $75
   - [x] $1,050
   - $10,500
   > The first costs $75 and the second $1,125. The difference is $1,050.

14. {#bk14} [calc] A fund returned 9.40% and its index returned 9.55%. What is the tracking difference?
   - +0.15 percentage points
   - [x] −0.15 percentage points
   - −9.40 percentage points
   - −1.5 percentage points
   > Tracking difference = fund return − index return = 9.40% − 9.55% = −0.15 points.

15. {#bk15} How is an ETF's expense ratio charged to the investor?
   - It is billed to the investor each month
   - It is charged once at purchase
   - It is paid only when the ETF is sold
   - [x] It is deducted gradually from the fund's assets, which shows up as slightly lower returns
   > Investors never see a bill for the fee. It is built into the fund's returns.

16. {#bk16} What is tracking difference?
   - The difference between bid and ask prices
   - [x] The gap between the ETF's return and its index's return over a period
   - The fund's yearly expense ratio
   - The difference between two ETFs' prices
   > Tracking difference is usually close to the expense ratio but is affected by other items such as holding a sample of securities.

17. {#bk17} Over a long holding period, which cost tends to matter most for an ETF?
   - The bid-ask spread, because it is paid every day
   - The commission on the first trade
   - Nothing, since costs vanish over time
   - [x] The expense ratio, since it is paid every year while the spread is paid when trading
   > Over a short period the spread can dominate. Over many years the yearly fee adds up.
