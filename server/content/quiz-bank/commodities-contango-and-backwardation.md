# Quiz

1. {#q1} What does it mean for a commodity futures curve to be in contango?
   - [x] Further-dated contracts are priced higher than near-dated ones
   - Further-dated contracts are priced lower than near-dated ones
   - All contracts are priced identically regardless of maturity
   - The commodity cannot be traded via futures at all
   > Contango describes a curve where later-dated contracts cost more than near-dated ones, in contrast to backwardation, where the opposite holds.

2. {#q2} How do storage costs push a commodity's futures curve toward contango?
   - [x] The cost of storing the commodity until a later date gets built into later contracts' prices, pushing them higher
   - Storage costs have no effect on futures pricing
   - Storage costs only affect the spot price, never futures prices
   - Storage costs always push the curve into backwardation instead
   > Physically holding a commodity until a later delivery date costs money, and that cost is reflected in a higher price for later-dated contracts — the classic driver of contango.

3. {#q3} What is convenience yield, and how does it affect the futures curve?
   - [x] The benefit of holding the physical commodity right now, which can pull the curve into backwardation when it's high
   - A fee charged for storing a commodity
   - It only applies to financial futures, never physical commodities
   - Convenience yield always pushes the curve toward contango
   > When having the physical commodity on hand right now is especially valuable, that convenience yield can outweigh storage costs and pull the curve into backwardation.

4. {#q4} Why don't financial futures, like stock index futures, experience the same contango/backwardation dynamics from storage and convenience yield?
   - [x] There's no physical asset to store and no convenience benefit to holding it right now, since it's not a physical good
   - Financial futures always trade in backwardation for unrelated reasons
   - Financial futures curves behave in exactly the same way as commodity curves
   - Stock index futures have higher storage costs than physical commodities
   > The storage-cost and convenience-yield forces are specific to physical goods — a financial futures contract has neither, which is why its curve behaves differently.

5. {#q5} What happens when convenience yield outweighs storage costs?
   - [x] The curve can be pulled into backwardation
   - The curve is always pushed further into contango
   - The futures contract stops trading entirely
   - Storage costs and convenience yield can never offset each other
   > When the benefit of holding the physical commodity now outweighs the cost of storing it for later delivery, the curve tends toward backwardation rather than contango.

6. {#calc1} [calc] Crude is $82 a barrel. Storing, insuring and financing a barrel costs $0.75 a month. What does the cost of carry imply for the 3-month futures price?
   - $82.75
   - $86.50
   - [x] $84.25
   - $79.75
   > The carry for 3 months is 3 × $0.75 = $2.25, so the fair futures price is $82 + $2.25 = $84.25, a market in contango.

7. {#calc2} [calc] Spot crude is $82 and the 3-month future is $84.25. What is the cost of carry as an annual percentage (simple)?
   - [x] About 11%
   - About 2.7%
   - About 2.74% a month
   - About 5.5%
   > The carry over 3 months is $2.25 / $82 ≈ 2.74%, and four quarters make about 11% a year.

8. {#bk1} [calc] Spot oil is $75, the 3-month future is $77, and the 6-month future is $79. What shape is the curve, and what is the 6-month annualized slope relative to spot?
   - Backwardation, about 10.7%
   - [x] Contango, about 10.7%
   - Contango, about 5.3%
   - Backwardation, about 5.3%
   > Futures above spot is contango. The 6-month slope is ($79 − $75) / $75 = 5.3% over six months, or about 10.7% annualized.

9. {#bk2} [calc] Natural gas spot is $4.00 and the 3-month future is $3.70. What is the 3-month basis (spot minus futures) and the curve shape?
   - $0.30, contango
   - −$0.30, backwardation
   - $7.70, contango
   - [x] $0.30, backwardation
   > Spot minus futures is $4.00 − $3.70 = $0.30. Futures below spot is backwardation.

10. {#bk3} [calc] Gold spot is $1,950 and the 1-year future is $2,040. Assuming the gap is pure cost of carry, what is the implied annual carry cost?
   - 9.0%
   - [x] 4.6%
   - 0.46%
   - 2.3%
   > ($2,040 − $1,950) / $1,950 = 4.6% a year of financing and storage.
