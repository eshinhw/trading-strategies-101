# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A cereal maker needs 50,000 bushels of corn in 6 months and fears a price rise. Spot is $5.80 and the forward is $6.10. If spot at delivery is $6.60, how much does the forward save versus buying at spot then?
   - $15,000
   - $40,000
   - [x] $25,000
   - $5,000
   > The forward fixes $6.10 instead of $6.60, saving $0.50 × 50,000 = $25,000.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A bakery agrees to buy 30,000 bushels of wheat forward at $6.60. At delivery the market price is $7.20. How much does the forward save the bakery?
   - [x] $18,000
   - $198,000
   - $1,800
   - $216,000
   > The bakery pays $0.60 less per bushel than the market: $0.60 × 30,000 = $18,000.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A bakery agrees to buy 30,000 bushels of wheat forward at $6.60. At delivery the market price is $6.10. How much more does it pay than it would have at the market?
   - $18,000
   - $198,000
   - [x] $15,000
   - $1,500
   > The bakery pays $0.50 more per bushel: $0.50 × 30,000 = $15,000. That is the price of certainty.

4. {#bk4} [calc] An importer owes €1.5 million in 90 days and locks in a forward rate of 1.0950 dollars per euro. How many dollars will it pay?
   - $1,369,863
   - [x] $1,642,500
   - $1,500,000
   - $16,425
   > €1,500,000 × 1.0950 = $1,642,500.

5. {#bk5} [calc] A farmer expects 5,000 bushels but signs a forward to sell 10,000 bushels at $6.30. At delivery the price is $6.70. What is the loss on the 5,000 bushels the farmer does not own?
   - $4,000
   - $20,000
   - $200
   - [x] $2,000
   > The extra 5,000 bushels are an unhedged short: ($6.70 − $6.30) × 5,000 = $2,000. A tailor-made forward avoids this by matching the exact quantity.

6. {#bk6} What do both sides of a forward get in exchange for giving up the chance to benefit if the market moves in their favour?
   - A guaranteed profit
   - [x] Certainty about the price they will pay or receive
   - Protection from counterparty default
   - The right to cancel at any time
   > Whichever way the market moves, one party would have been better off waiting. Both accept that for a known price.

7. {#bk7} Why does a farmer with 5,000 bushels arriving in October benefit from a tailor-made forward?
   - It makes the farmer's crop sell at a higher price
   - It guarantees that spot prices will not fall
   - It removes the farmer's obligation to deliver
   - [x] It can be sized and dated to the exact exposure, instead of being forced into a standard contract
   > A forward can match the real quantity, date, quality and location, avoiding a mismatch that would leave part of the exposure unhedged or over-hedged.

8. {#bk8} How do forwards relate to other derivatives such as futures?
   - Futures came first and forwards were created later to replace them
   - Forwards and futures are unrelated
   - [x] A forward is the simplest derivative and futures were built on it by standardizing the contract and routing it through an exchange
   - A forward is a futures contract with a premium
   > Agreements to deliver later at a price set today are among the oldest financial contracts. Exchanges later standardized them as futures.
