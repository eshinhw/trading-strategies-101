# Quiz

1. {#q1} Which of these is NOT one of the three basic reasons people trade currencies?
   - Transacting, to pay for or receive something in another currency
   - Hedging, to reduce currency risk
   - Speculating or investing, to profit from currency moves
   - [x] Printing new currency to fund government spending
   > Currency trading is used to transact, hedge, or speculate and invest, while printing money is a government function, not a market activity.

2. {#q2} Why would an importer buy a foreign currency?
   - [x] To settle an invoice owed in that currency, which is a transaction need rather than a bet on the exchange rate
   - To speculate that the exchange rate will fall
   - Because buying currency is required by law every month
   - To avoid ever paying the supplier
   > Paying a foreign supplier requires the supplier's currency, which is simply the mechanics of cross-border payment.

3. {#q3} What is the main trade-off of hedging a currency exposure?
   - Hedging always increases profit
   - Hedging has no cost and no downside
   - [x] It makes a future amount more certain, but gives up the benefit if the exchange rate moves in your favor
   - Hedging guarantees a profit on every trade
   > A hedge removes uncertainty in both directions, which protects against losses but also forgoes gains.

4. {#q4} In a speculative or investing use of currencies, how does the currency exposure differ from a hedge?
   - It is an accidental side effect
   - It is always zero
   - It is illegal
   - [x] It is the point of the trade, taken on deliberately in hope of a return
   > A speculator or investor wants currency exposure and takes it on purpose, while a hedger is trying to remove an exposure they already have.

5. {#q5} A trader sells euros forward but has no euros to receive. Compared with a company hedging a euro receivable, what is the trade?
   - A different trade with a different price
   - Impossible to place
   - [x] Economically the same trade, but it creates new currency risk rather than offsetting existing risk
   - A trade with no risk at all
   > The trade can be identical, and what changes is the surrounding position: a hedger offsets an existing exposure while a speculator creates a new one.

6. {#calc1} [calc] A company expects €1,500,000 and sells it forward at 1.1055. At maturity the spot rate is 1.1500. What is the company's cost or benefit compared with not hedging?
   - It gains $66,750
   - It gives up $44,500
   - [x] It gives up $66,750
   - It gains $44,500
   > Hedged: 1,500,000 × 1.1055 = $1,658,250. Unhedged: 1,500,000 × 1.15 = $1,725,000. The hedge gives up $66,750 of upside in exchange for certainty.
