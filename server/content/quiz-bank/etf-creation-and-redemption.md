# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] An ETF's creation unit is 50,000 shares and each share's net asset value is $40. What is the value of one creation unit?
   - $200,000
   - $20,000,000
   - [x] $2,000,000
   - $50,000
   > Creation unit value = 50,000 × $40 = $2,000,000.

2. {#bk2} [calc] An ETF has a net asset value of $100 per share and trades at $100.50. What is the premium or discount?
   - [x] 0.50% premium
   - 0.50% discount
   - 5.00% premium
   - 0.25% premium
   > ($100.50 − $100) / $100 = +0.50%, a premium.

3. {#bk3} [calc] An ETF has a net asset value of $50 per share and trades at $49.40. What is the premium or discount?
   - 1.20% premium
   - 12.00% discount
   - [x] 1.20% discount
   - 0.60% discount
   > ($49.40 − $50) / $50 = −1.20%, a discount.

4. {#bk4} [calc] An ETF has a net asset value of $25 per share and trades at $25.30. What is the premium or discount?
   - 1.20% discount
   - [x] 1.20% premium
   - 12.00% premium
   - 0.60% premium
   > ($25.30 − $25) / $25 = +1.20%, a premium.

5. {#bk5} [calc] An authorized participant sees an ETF trading at $50.40 while its basket is worth $50.00 per share. The creation unit is 100,000 shares. Ignoring costs, what is the arbitrage profit from creating and selling a unit?
   - $4,000
   - $400,000
   - $50,400
   - [x] $40,000
   > The participant buys the basket at $50.00, delivers it for new ETF shares, and sells them at $50.40, earning $0.40 × 100,000 = $40,000.

6. {#bk6} [calc] An ETF trades at $50.30 while its underlying basket is worth $50.00 per ETF share. A creation unit is 50,000 shares and the participant's costs are $2,500. What is the net profit from creating a unit?
   - $15,000
   - [x] $12,500
   - $2,500
   - $17,500
   > The gap is $0.30 × 50,000 = $15,000. After $2,500 of costs the profit is $12,500.

7. {#bk7} [calc] An ETF trades at $49.60 against a basket worth $50.00 per share. A unit is 50,000 shares. Ignoring costs, what does a participant make by buying ETF shares and redeeming them for the basket?
   - $0.40
   - $2,480,000
   - $2,500
   - [x] $20,000
   > The ETF is $0.40 cheaper than its basket. $0.40 × 50,000 = $20,000.

8. {#bk8} [calc] A creation unit is 25,000 shares and the ETF's NAV is $80. What is the value of the basket for one creation unit?
   - $200,000
   - $20,000
   - [x] $2,000,000
   - $25,000
   > 25,000 × $80 = $2,000,000.

9. {#bk9} [calc] An ETF's shares outstanding grow from 400,000 to 550,000 and each creation unit is 50,000 shares. How many creation units were created, net?
   - [x] 3
   - 2
   - 150
   - 11
   > The increase is 150,000 shares. 150,000 / 50,000 = 3 units.

10. {#bk10} [calc] Arbitrage costs are $0.05 per share on an ETF with a NAV of $50. What premium to NAV is needed before creating shares becomes profitable?
   - More than 1%
   - More than 0.05%
   - More than 5%
   - [x] More than 0.10%
   > $0.05 / $50 = 0.10%. The premium must exceed that to cover costs.

11. {#bk11} [calc] A participant earns $12,500 on a creation unit worth $2,500,000 at NAV. What is the profit as a percentage of the unit's value?
   - 5%
   - [x] 0.5%
   - 0.05%
   - 12.5%
   > $12,500 / $2,500,000 = 0.5%.

12. {#bk12} [calc] A participant redeems 50,000 ETF shares with a NAV of $50. How much does the value of the fund's assets fall?
   - $50,000
   - $250,000
   - $25,000,000
   - [x] $2,500,000
   > 50,000 × $50 = $2,500,000 of securities leave the fund.

13. {#bk13} Who creates and redeems ETF shares?
   - Any individual investor on the exchange
   - [x] Authorized participants, large institutional intermediaries, working with the ETF issuer
   - The exchange itself
   - The fund's retail customers each day
   > Only authorized participants can deal directly with the issuer in creation units.

14. {#bk14} What does an authorized participant deliver to create new ETF shares?
   - Only cash to the exchange
   - Existing ETF shares for a fee
   - [x] The basket of underlying securities, or sometimes cash, in exchange for a block of new ETF shares
   - A promise to buy shares later
   > The basket goes to the issuer and a creation unit of new ETF shares comes back.

15. {#bk15} How does creation and redemption keep an ETF's price near its NAV?
   - [x] Participants profit from any gap by buying the cheaper side and creating or redeeming, which pushes the prices together
   - The issuer sets the price by decree
   - Trading is halted whenever a gap appears
   - The ETF's price is calculated from NAV with no trading
   > If the ETF is above its basket value, participants create shares and sell them. If below, they buy and redeem.
