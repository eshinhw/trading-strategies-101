# Quiz

1. {#q1} Why are equity index futures cash-settled rather than physically settled?
   - [x] There is no single physical asset corresponding to a broad stock index to deliver
   - Cash settlement is required by law for all futures contracts
   - Equity index futures are never actually settled
   - Physical settlement would be cheaper, so cash settlement is a workaround
   > A stock index is a calculated number representing many underlying stocks, not a single deliverable asset, so cash settlement — paying the difference — is the only practical option.

2. {#q2} Who typically uses currency (FX) futures to hedge?
   - [x] Exporters and importers managing foreign-currency receivables and payables
   - Only central banks
   - Only companies that never do business internationally
   - FX futures cannot be used for hedging purposes
   > A company expecting to receive or pay a foreign currency in the future is exactly the kind of exposure FX futures are used to hedge.

3. {#q3} Which futures markets are among the oldest, and historically the reason major exchanges were created?
   - [x] Agricultural futures (corn, wheat, soybeans, cattle, coffee)
   - Cryptocurrency futures
   - Equity index futures
   - Interest rate futures
   > Agricultural futures predate most other categories, tracing back to exchanges originally set up to manage the price risk of farming and feeding the world.

4. {#q4} What do cryptocurrency futures let a trader do?
   - [x] Gain long or short exposure to cryptocurrency prices without directly holding and custodying the underlying coins
   - Only buy cryptocurrency, never sell it short
   - Avoid all regulation, since crypto futures trade off-exchange
   - Convert cryptocurrency directly into physical gold
   > Trading a futures contract on Bitcoin or Ether gives exposure to the price without the operational burden of actually custodying the underlying cryptocurrency.

5. {#q5} An E-mini S&P 500 contract at 4,500 ($50 multiplier) and a crude oil contract at $80 (1,000 barrels) have very different notional sizes. Why does this matter?
   - It doesn't — all futures contracts carry identical risk
   - [x] Because position sizing and risk have to be worked out market by market, since each contract controls a very different dollar amount of exposure
   - Because only crude oil futures are traded by real institutions
   - Because the E-mini S&P is always the riskier contract
   > $225,000 versus $80,000 of notional exposure per contract is a large difference — a trader has to account for each market's actual contract size, not assume one size fits all.

6. {#calc1} [calc] Gold futures are $2,000 an ounce and each contract is 100 ounces. How many contracts give $600,000 of exposure?
   - 30
   - [x] 3
   - 0.3
   - 6
   > Each contract is 100 × $2,000 = $200,000, and $600,000 / $200,000 = 3 contracts.

7. {#bk1} [calc] A trader buys 7 gold contracts (100 ounces each) at 1,960, and the price later moves to 2,004. What is the profit or loss on the position?
   - +$308
   - +$4,400
   - [x] +$30,800
   - −$30,800
   > The move is 2,004 − 1,960 = 44.00 per unit, and 44.00 × 100 × 7 contracts = $30,800.

8. {#bk2} [calc] A trader buys 2 copper contracts (25,000 pounds each) at 4.10, and the price later moves to 4.34. What is the profit or loss on the position?
   - [x] +$12,000
   - +$0.48
   - +$6,000
   - −$12,000
   > The move is 4.34 − 4.10 = 0.24 per unit, and 0.24 × 25,000 × 2 contracts = $12,000.

9. {#bk3} [calc] A gold future covers 100 ounces and gold is at $2,000. What is the contract's notional value?
   - $2,000
   - $20,000
   - [x] $200,000
   - $2,000,000
   > Notional = price × contract size = $2,000 × 100 = $200,000.

10. {#bk4} [calc] The E-mini S&P 500 has a multiplier of $50 and the Micro E-mini has a multiplier of $5. How many micro contracts equal the exposure of one E-mini?
   - 5
   - [x] 10
   - 50
   - 100
   > $50 / $5 = 10 micro contracts per E-mini.

11. {#bk5} [calc] A natural gas contract covers 10,000 MMBtu and gas trades at $3.40. The margin is $3,000 per contract. What is the margin as a percentage of notional?
   - 11.3%
   - 0.9%
   - 88%
   - [x] 8.8%
   > Notional = 10,000 × $3.40 = $34,000. Margin / notional = $3,000 / $34,000 = 8.8%.

12. {#bk6} [calc] A crude oil contract (1,000 barrels) is at $80 with $6,400 of margin. What leverage does the margin give (notional divided by margin)?
   - 8 times
   - [x] 12.5 times
   - 80 times
   - 1.25 times
   > Notional = $80,000. Leverage = $80,000 / $6,400 = 12.5 times.
