# Quiz

1. {#q1} What does an ETF share represent?
   - [x] A proportional claim on a basket of underlying assets held by the fund
   - A direct loan to a single company
   - Ownership of exactly one stock, chosen by the investor
   - A guarantee of a fixed annual return
   > An ETF pools many underlying assets into one fund, and each share represents a proportional slice of that whole basket, not a single holding.

2. {#q2} How does ETF trading differ from a traditional mutual fund?
   - [x] An ETF trades continuously on an exchange throughout the day, while a mutual fund is only priced and traded once per day
   - Mutual funds trade continuously, while ETFs only trade once per day
   - There is no difference between the two
   - ETFs cannot be bought or sold by individual investors
   > ETF shares trade intraday on an exchange like a stock, in contrast to a mutual fund, which is only transacted once a day at a single end-of-day price.

3. {#q3} What do most ETFs aim to do relative to an index?
   - [x] Replicate the index's return as closely as possible, rather than trying to beat it
   - Guarantee a return significantly higher than the index
   - Ignore any index entirely and pick stocks at random
   - Short the index at all times
   > Most ETFs are passively managed to track a specific index or market segment, aiming for close replication rather than active outperformance.

4. {#q4} What combination of features has driven ETFs' popularity?
   - [x] Fund-like diversification, stock-like intraday liquidity, and typically lower cost than active management
   - Guaranteed profits with no risk
   - Being available only to institutional investors
   - Requiring investors to pick individual stocks themselves
   > ETFs blend diversification, liquidity, and low cost in one product, which is the core reason they've grown so much since the early 1990s.

5. {#q5} What kinds of assets can an ETF's underlying basket hold?
   - Only individual company stocks, and nothing else
   - [x] Stocks, bonds, commodities, or a mix, depending on the ETF
   - Only physical gold
   - ETFs never actually hold any underlying assets
   > An ETF's underlying basket can be built from stocks, bonds, commodities, or a combination, depending on what the fund is designed to track.

6. {#calc1} [calc] An ETF share trades at $400 and a company makes up 3.5% of the fund. How many dollars of that company does one share represent?
   - $3.50
   - [x] $14
   - $140
   - $0.035
   > 3.5% of $400 = $14.

7. {#bk1} [calc] An ETF has $480 million in assets, $12 million in liabilities, and 9.36 million shares outstanding. What is its net asset value per share?
   - [x] $50.00
   - $51.28
   - $48.72
   - $5.00
   > NAV = (assets − liabilities) / shares = ($480 million − $12 million) / 9.36 million = $50.00.

8. {#bk2} [calc] An ETF holds 100 shares of Stock A at $50, 200 shares of Stock B at $20 and 50 shares of Stock C at $80, with no liabilities. It has 100 ETF shares outstanding. What is its net asset value per share?
   - $13,000
   - $100
   - [x] $130
   - $40
   > Assets = $5,000 + $4,000 + $4,000 = $13,000. NAV per share = $13,000 / 100 = $130.

9. {#bk3} [calc] An ETF tracks an index that is at 4,500 and its share price is one-tenth of the index level, $450. The index rises 2%. What is the ETF's approximate price?
   - [x] $459
   - $452
   - $4,590
   - $470
   > A 2% rise takes $450 to $450 × 1.02 = $459.

10. {#bk4} [calc] An ETF holds 500 stocks in equal weights. One stock falls 50%. What is the approximate effect on the ETF?
   - −0.5%
   - −50%
   - −1%
   - [x] −0.1%
   > Each stock is 1/500 = 0.2% of the fund. A 50% fall costs 0.2% × 50% = 0.1%.

11. {#bk5} [calc] An ETF priced at $90 pays $1.80 a year in distributions. What is its distribution yield?
   - 5%
   - [x] 2%
   - 1.8%
   - 0.02%
   > $1.80 / $90 = 2%.

12. {#bk6} How does an ETF's trading differ from a traditional mutual fund's?
   - ETF shares trade once a day, while a mutual fund trades continuously
   - Both are priced only after markets close
   - ETF shares can only be bought from the fund company
   - [x] ETF shares trade throughout the day on an exchange, while a mutual fund is priced and traded once a day after the close
   > ETFs trade like stocks at whatever price buyers and sellers agree to at that moment.

13. {#bk7} An ETF is designed to track the S&P 500. What is its goal for its return?
   - Beat it by a large margin every year
   - [x] Replicate its return as closely as possible rather than beat it
   - Hold the opposite positions
   - Ignore it and pick stocks freely
   > Most ETFs track an index or defined slice of the market, using a rules-based basket.

14. {#bk8} Which three features combine to explain why ETFs became so popular?
   - [x] The diversification of a fund, the intraday liquidity of a stock, and typically lower cost than active mutual funds
   - Guaranteed returns, no fees and no risk
   - Government backing, fixed prices and daily payouts
   - Only one holding, high fees and monthly trading
   > ETFs offer broad exposure that trades like a stock, usually at a low cost.
