# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A company has 2.5 billion shares outstanding at $84 each. What is its market capitalization?
   - $2,100 billion
   - [x] $210 billion
   - $21 billion
   - $420 billion
   > Market cap = shares outstanding × share price = 2,500,000,000 × $84 = $210 billion.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A company has 480 million shares outstanding at $35 each. What is its market capitalization?
   - $168 billion
   - $1.7 billion
   - $33.6 billion
   - [x] $16.8 billion
   > Market cap = shares outstanding × share price = 480,000,000 × $35 = $16.8 billion.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A company has 60 million shares outstanding at $22.50 each. What is its market capitalization?
   - $13.5 billion
   - [x] $1.35 billion
   - $135 million
   - $2.7 billion
   > Market cap = shares outstanding × share price = 60,000,000 × $22.50 = $1.35 billion.

4. {#bk4} [calc] A market-cap-weighted fund holds Stock A ($300 billion market cap) and Stock B ($100 billion market cap) and nothing else. What is Stock B's weight?
   - 50%
   - 33%
   - [x] 25%
   - 10%
   > Weights follow market cap: $100 billion / ($300 billion + $100 billion) = 25%.

5. {#bk5} [calc] A company has 200 million shares and the price rises from $50 to $56. By how much does its market cap rise?
   - [x] $1.2 billion
   - $6
   - $12 billion
   - $120 million
   > The gain per share is $6. $6 × 200 million = $1.2 billion.

6. {#bk6} [calc] A portfolio holds 30% technology (return +10%), 20% energy (−5%) and 50% healthcare (+2%). What is the portfolio return?
   - 2.3%
   - 7%
   - [x] 3%
   - 1.0%
   > 0.30 × 10% + 0.20 × (−5%) + 0.50 × 2% = 3.0% − 1.0% + 1.0% = 3.0%.

7. {#bk7} [calc] A company has a market cap of $12 billion and 300 million shares outstanding. What is its share price?
   - [x] $40
   - $4
   - $400
   - $36
   > $12,000,000,000 / 300,000,000 = $40.

8. {#bk8} A company has 50 million shares outstanding trading at $20 each. What does its market capitalization measure?
   - The amount of cash the company holds, $50 million here
   - [x] The market's current valuation of the entire company, $1 billion here
   - The company's annual profit, $20 million here
   - The value of the company's assets, whatever the price
   > Market cap is share price times shares outstanding: $20 × 50 million = $1 billion, a single number for the whole company.

9. {#bk9} What is the purpose of a stock index such as the S&P 500?
   - It guarantees returns to every stock it holds
   - It sets the price of each stock in it
   - It lists only companies that pay dividends
   - [x] It tracks a defined basket of stocks to represent a market or slice of it, serving as a benchmark
   > Indices, often weighted by market cap, give investors a yardstick to judge individual stocks, sectors and funds.
