# Quiz

1. {#q1} What role do market makers play in stock trading?
   - [x] They continuously quote a bid and an ask price, providing the liquidity that lets orders get filled quickly
   - They set the company's earnings reports
   - They vote on behalf of all shareholders
   - They determine a company's dividend policy
   > Market makers stand ready to buy at their quoted bid and sell at their quoted ask, which is what provides the liquidity underlying an exchange's order matching.

2. {#q2} What is the key difference between a market order and a limit order?
   - A market order guarantees a specific price but not speed; a limit order guarantees speed but not price
   - [x] A market order fills immediately at the best available price, prioritizing speed; a limit order specifies a price and may not fill at all
   - They are identical in every respect
   - Limit orders can only be used to sell, never to buy
   > A market order trades execution certainty for price uncertainty, while a limit order trades price certainty (if it fills) for execution uncertainty.

3. {#q3} What does it mean to "go long" a stock?
   - Borrowing shares and selling them immediately
   - [x] Buying the stock outright, profiting if the price rises and losing if it falls
   - Holding a stock for at least ten years
   - Betting that the stock's price will fall
   > Going long is simply the ordinary sense of buying a stock — you own it outright, and your position gains value as the price rises.

4. {#q4} How does selling short work?
   - [x] Borrowing shares, selling them immediately, and aiming to buy them back later at a lower price to return to the lender
   - Buying a stock and holding it for a very short period of time
   - Selling a stock you already own and never buying it back
   - A type of dividend payment
   > Short selling borrows shares to sell first, with the plan to repurchase them later at a lower price — profiting from a price decline rather than a rise.

5. {#q5} Why does a short seller face theoretically unlimited losses, unlike a long position?
   - [x] Because there's no cap on how high a stock's price can rise, while a long position's maximum loss is capped at what was paid for the shares
   - Short selling is actually less risky than going long
   - Short sellers are protected by a guaranteed maximum loss limit
   - A stock's price can never rise once it has been sold short
   > A long position's downside is capped at the purchase price, since a stock can't fall below zero, but a short position's losses grow without limit as the price keeps rising, since there's no ceiling on how high it can go.

6. {#calc1} [calc] A trader shorts 200 shares at $40 and buys them back at $46. What is the result?
   - [x] A $1,200 loss
   - A $1,200 gain
   - A $6 loss
   - An $800 loss
   > The loss is ($46 − $40) × 200 shares = $1,200, since the trader sold at $40 and had to buy back higher.

7. {#bk1} [calc] A stock is quoted 24.98 bid and 25.02 ask. An investor buys 2,000 shares at the ask and sells them immediately at the bid. What is the round-trip loss?
   - $40
   - $160
   - $8
   - [x] $80
   > The loss is the spread on every share: (25.02 − 24.98) × 2,000 = $80.

8. {#bk2} [calc] A stock is quoted 101.10 bid and 101.30 ask. An investor buys 500 shares at the ask and sells them immediately at the bid. What is the round-trip loss?
   - $50
   - [x] $100
   - $200
   - $10
   > The loss is the spread on every share: (101.30 − 101.10) × 500 = $100.

9. {#bk3} [calc] A stock is quoted 9.40 bid and 9.46 ask. An investor buys 10,000 shares at the ask and sells them immediately at the bid. What is the round-trip loss?
   - $300
   - $1,200
   - $60
   - [x] $600
   > The loss is the spread on every share: (9.46 − 9.40) × 10,000 = $600.

10. {#bk4} [calc] An investor has $20,000 in a margin account and buys stock worth $40,000 using the maximum 2:1 buying power. If the stock then falls 10%, what is the investor's equity?
   - [x] $16,000
   - $18,000
   - $36,000
   - $4,000
   > The position drops 10% × $40,000 = $4,000. Equity is $20,000 − $4,000 = $16,000, a 20% loss on the investor's own money.

11. {#bk5} [calc] A limit order to buy 300 shares at $42.00 is placed while the stock trades at $42.40. The stock falls to $41.90 and the order fills. How much is paid in total at best?
   - $12,720
   - $12,570
   - [x] $12,600
   - $12,630
   > A buy limit order fills at the limit price or better, so at best the order pays $42.00 × 300 = $12,600 (it fills at or below $42.00).

12. {#bk6} [calc] An investor shorts 200 shares at $50 and covers at $42. What is the profit, ignoring costs?
   - [x] $1,600
   - $8
   - $8,400
   - −$1,600
   > The investor sells at $50 and buys back at $42: ($50 − $42) × 200 = $1,600.

13. {#bk7} [calc] An investor shorts 200 shares at $50 and the stock rises to $63 before being covered. What is the loss?
   - $1,600
   - $10,000
   - [x] $2,600
   - $13
   > The short loses what the price rises: ($63 − $50) × 200 = $2,600. A short's possible loss is unlimited.

14. {#bk8} [calc] A short seller borrows 1,000 shares at $30 for 90 days and pays an annual borrow fee of 2% (365-day basis). What is the borrow cost?
   - $600
   - $1,500
   - $14.80
   - [x] $148
   > Position value = $30,000. Fee = $30,000 × 2% × 90 / 365 = $148.

15. {#bk9} [calc] An investor places a limit order to sell at $31.00 and the best bid is $31.05. At what price does the order fill?
   - $31.00
   - [x] $31.05
   - $31.025
   - The order does not fill
   > A limit order fills at the limit price or better, so a sell at $31.00 fills against the $31.05 bid.

16. {#bk10} [calc] An investor buys 300 shares at $41.20 and sells at $42.05, paying a $5 commission on each trade. What is the net profit?
   - $255
   - $265
   - [x] $245
   - $250
   > Gross profit = ($42.05 − $41.20) × 300 = $255. Commissions are $10. Net = $245.

17. {#bk11} What is the main difference between a market order and a limit order?
   - [x] A market order executes immediately at the best available price, while a limit order sets the worst price the investor will accept
   - A market order sets the worst acceptable price, while a limit order executes immediately
   - They are identical
   - A limit order is always filled first
   > A market order prioritizes speed over price certainty. A limit order prioritizes price over speed and may not fill.

18. {#bk12} Why is the potential loss on a short sale of stock described as unlimited?
   - Short sellers must always pay the full price of the stock in cash
   - Short positions always lose money
   - [x] The price can rise without a ceiling, while a long position can lose at most what was paid
   - The exchange does not allow short sellers to buy back shares
   > A stock bought outright can fall only to zero. A stock sold short can keep rising, so the loss has no upper limit.

19. {#bk13} What do market makers do on a stock exchange?
   - [x] Continuously quote both a price they will buy at and a price they will sell at
   - Decide which companies may list shares
   - Guarantee that every stock will rise
   - Set the price of a stock each morning
   > Market makers supply constant two-sided quotes, so a buyer or a seller can usually find someone to trade with.
