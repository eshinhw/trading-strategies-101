# Quiz

1. {#q1} How does a plan-shaping distressed investor go beyond simply negotiating over an existing plan proposal?
   - [x] The investor actively helps draft the plan's actual structure, rather than just reacting to a proposal
   - It involves no participation in the reorganization at all
   - It is identical to the purely passive buy-and-hold strategy
   - It only ever involves rejecting every proposed plan with no alternative
   > Plan-shaping investors move from reacting to a proposal to actively authoring the terms of the restructuring itself.

2. {#q2} What is a central design choice in most reorganization plans?
   - [x] How much of each debt class converts into equity, and at what implied valuation
   - The company's future advertising budget
   - Reorganization plans never involve any debt-to-equity decisions
   - The color scheme of the company's new logo
   > The debt-for-equity conversion terms are one of the most consequential decisions in shaping how value is ultimately distributed among creditor classes.

3. {#q3} What is "exit financing"?
   - [x] New financing a company needs to operate going forward as it emerges from bankruptcy
   - A fee charged to creditors for leaving a creditors' committee
   - A type of tax owed only by companies that avoid bankruptcy entirely
   - Exit financing has no relevance to a reorganization plan
   > Exit financing funds the reorganized company's ongoing operations, and providing or arranging it is itself a source of investor influence over the plan.

4. {#q4} What happens when more than one creditor group proposes a competing plan of reorganization?
   - [x] The court and voting creditors ultimately decide between the competing plans
   - Competing plans are automatically illegal and immediately rejected
   - Only the company itself is ever allowed to propose a plan
   - Competing plans are combined into one plan automatically with no vote
   > The formal voting and court-confirmation process is what resolves competing plan proposals, giving a well-structured alternative plan a real chance of winning.

5. {#q5} A plan-shaping investor proposes an alternative reorganization plan. Which combination would most help it win creditor votes?
   - [x] Better recovery terms for junior creditors and a commitment to provide the company's exit financing
   - A guarantee of zero recovery for all creditors
   - An immediate liquidation of the entire company
   - A plan with no changes from the original proposal
   > Combining improved terms with a financing commitment is exactly the kind of package a plan-shaping investor uses to make their proposal more attractive than the alternative.

6. {#calc1} [calc] A junior class of $50 million would recover 8 cents under the company's plan and 22 cents under an investor's alternative plan. How much more does the class receive under the alternative plan?
   - $11 million
   - $4 million
   - [x] $7 million
   - $14 million
   > The difference is 22 − 8 = 14 cents on the dollar, and 14% × $50M = $7M.

7. {#calc2} [calc] An investor provides $40 million of exit financing at 11%. How much interest does the company pay each year?
   - [x] $4.4 million
   - $0.44 million
   - $44 million
   - $29.1 million
   > $40,000,000 × 11% = $4,400,000.

8. {#bk1} [calc] A company produces EBITDA of $20 million and comparable firms trade at 6 times EBITDA. What is its estimated enterprise value?
   - $3.3 million
   - [x] $120 million
   - $26 million
   - $14 million
   > Enterprise value = EBITDA × multiple = $20 million × 6 = $120 million.

9. {#bk2} [calc] In a reorganization, creditors receive 80% of the new equity, valued at $90 million. What is the value of the creditors' share?
   - $90 million
   - $18 million
   - $112 million
   - [x] $72 million
   > 80% × $90 million = $72 million.
