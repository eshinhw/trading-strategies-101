# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] In a futures market, hedgers hold 120,000 short contracts and 80,000 long contracts, and open interest is 400,000. What is hedgers' net short position as a share of open interest?
   - 30%
   - 50%
   - [x] 10%
   - 40,000
   > Net short = 120,000 − 80,000 = 40,000. As a share of open interest: 40,000 / 400,000 = 10%.

2. {#bk2} [calc] Hedgers are net short 40,000 contracts. Speculators take the other side of the whole market. What is speculators' net position?
   - [x] Net long 40,000 contracts
   - Net short 40,000 contracts
   - Net long 80,000 contracts
   - Flat
   > For every futures contract, a short has a long. If hedgers are net short 40,000, speculators are net long 40,000.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A hedging-pressure strategy buys futures when hedgers are net short more than 5% of open interest. Hedgers were net short 4% last month and 7% this month. What does the rule do this month?
   - Sells, because 7% is above 5%
   - Does nothing, because the change is only 3%
   - [x] Buys, because 7% is above 5%
   - Buys, because the change is above 5%
   > The rule tests the level against 5%. At 7% it is above the trigger, so the rule buys.
