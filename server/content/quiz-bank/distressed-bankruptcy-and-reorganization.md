# Quiz

1. {#q1} What is the key difference between reorganization and liquidation?
   - [x] Reorganization restructures debt and continues operating as a going concern; liquidation sells off assets and ends the business
   - The two terms describe the exact same process
   - Liquidation always results in a larger payout to creditors than reorganization
   - Reorganization is only available to companies with no debt at all
   > Reorganization preserves the business as an operating entity, while liquidation winds it down — the choice generally depends on whether the underlying business is still viable.

2. {#q2} What does the automatic stay do when a company files for bankruptcy protection?
   - [x] It immediately halts individual creditors' collection efforts and lawsuits against the company
   - It immediately liquidates all of the company's assets
   - It has no practical effect on creditors at all
   - It automatically cancels every one of the company's debts
   > The stay is what gives the company room to negotiate a coordinated restructuring instead of being dismantled by the fastest-moving creditor.

3. {#q3} What does a plan of reorganization describe?
   - [x] How existing claims will be treated — reduced, converted to equity, or paid in some other form
   - Only the company's future marketing strategy
   - A plan of reorganization has no relationship to creditor claims
   - The exact daily stock price the company must maintain
   > The plan is the core document determining how each class of creditor's claim is ultimately treated as the company restructures.

4. {#q4} What frequently happens to former creditors once a company emerges from bankruptcy?
   - [x] They often end up holding equity in the reorganized company
   - They are always paid the full original face value of their debt in cash
   - They automatically lose all rights to any recovery
   - Former creditors have no possible role in the reorganized company
   > Debt-to-equity conversion is a common feature of reorganization plans, turning former lenders into the new owners — a mechanism central to several distressed-investing strategies.

5. {#q5} An over-indebted company with a sound business goes through a reorganization. What typically changes about it once it emerges from bankruptcy?
   - [x] Its capital structure — a lighter debt load, with some former bondholders now holding equity — while the underlying business is unchanged
   - The company ceases to exist entirely
   - Nothing changes at all about the company
   - The company's operations are sold off to a competitor
   > Reorganization is fundamentally a restructuring of who owns and is owed what, not necessarily a change in what the business actually does.

6. {#calc1} [calc] A company has $300 million of debt at 8% and EBITDA of $45 million. Half the debt is converted to equity. What is interest coverage (EBITDA / interest) afterwards?
   - 1.9 times
   - 7.5 times
   - 3.0 times
   - [x] 3.75 times
   > The remaining debt is $150M, so interest is $150M × 8% = $12M and coverage is $45M / $12M = 3.75 times (it was about 1.9 times before).

7. {#calc2} [calc] A company owes 8% a year on $100 million of debt, and the automatic stay pauses interest for 6 months. How much interest does the company avoid paying in that time?
   - $8 million
   - [x] $4 million
   - $0.4 million
   - $2 million
   > $100,000,000 × 8% × 0.5 = $4,000,000.

8. {#bk1} [calc] A reorganized company is worth $120 million. Secured debt of $70 million is repaid in full, and the remaining value goes to $100 million of unsecured debt. What percentage of unsecured debt is recovered?
   - 120%
   - [x] 50%
   - 70%
   - 30%
   > After the secured debt's $70 million, $50 million remains for unsecured creditors: $50 million / $100 million = 50%.

9. {#bk2} [calc] A bond with a face value of $1,000 recovers 30 cents on the dollar in bankruptcy. How much does a bondholder receive?
   - $700
   - $30
   - $3,000
   - [x] $300
   > Recovery = $1,000 × 0.30 = $300.
