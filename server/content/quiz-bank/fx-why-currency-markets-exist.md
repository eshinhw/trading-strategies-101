# Quiz

1. {#q1} What basic problem does the foreign exchange market solve?
   - Governments need a place to print new money
   - Stocks need a market where they can be listed
   - [x] People and businesses need to convert one currency into another to pay, earn, and invest across borders
   - Banks need somewhere to store gold
   > Because most economic activity crosses borders but most money is national, someone has to exchange currencies, and the FX market is where that happens.

2. {#q2} Which of these is a major source of everyday demand for currencies?
   - Domestic payroll taxes
   - [x] International trade, foreign investment, tourism, and money sent home by workers abroad
   - Local grocery purchases
   - Domestic savings accounts
   > Each of these cross-border activities requires exchanging one currency for another, which creates constant demand in the market.

3. {#q3} What changed about exchange rates in the early 1970s?
   - Every currency was permanently fixed to gold
   - Exchange rates stopped existing
   - Central banks stopped holding any foreign currency
   - [x] The Bretton Woods system broke down and most major currencies began to float, with rates set by supply and demand
   > After the U.S. suspended gold convertibility in 1971, major currencies moved to floating rates by 1973, which made exchange rates a market price.

4. {#q4} Why do some participants trade currencies while others are forced to?
   - [x] Corporations and travelers need a currency for real transactions, while funds and traders often choose to trade for hedging or profit
   - Only speculators are ever allowed to trade currencies
   - Central banks are the only participants who have a choice
   - Nobody ever has a choice
   > Participants range from those who must convert currency to do business to those who trade by choice, and that mix helps keep the market liquid.

5. {#q5} A German carmaker sells cars in the U.S. and is paid in dollars but pays its costs in euros. What does this illustrate?
   - [x] Routine business creates a constant need to convert currencies, and the exchange rate affects how many euros it receives
   - The company is speculating on the dollar
   - Exchange rates have no effect on companies
   - The company must hold dollars forever
   > Ordinary cross-border business requires currency conversion, which both drives the FX market and exposes the company to changes in the exchange rate.

6. {#calc1} [calc] A carmaker earns $20,000,000 and has €17,000,000 of costs. At EUR/USD 1.10, what is the monthly profit in euros, rounded to the nearest €0.1 million?
   - About €3.0 million
   - [x] About €1.2 million
   - About −€1.0 million
   - About €1.7 million
   > Revenue is $20,000,000 / 1.10 ≈ €18.2 million, and costs are €17.0 million, so the profit is about €1.2 million.
