# Quiz

1. {#q1} Which investor is best suited to selling a put?
   - An investor who expects the stock to collapse soon and wants to profit from the full extent of the decline
   - [x] One who is neutral to moderately bullish and would be happy to own the stock at the strike price
   - An investor who wants unlimited upside from a rally and is unwilling to accept any obligation to buy shares
   - An investor who never wants to own the stock at any price and wants a trade with no chance of assignment
   > A short put is paid to wait for a price at which the investor would like to buy.

2. {#q2} What is the main risk of selling a put?
   - The loss is limited to the premium received, no matter how far the stock falls, so there is no real risk in the trade
   - The loss is unlimited if the stock rises, because the seller of a put must deliver shares they do not own
   - [x] If the stock falls sharply, you must buy shares at the strike, so the loss can be large while profit is capped at the premium
   - A trader can never be assigned shares on a short put, so the premium is guaranteed income for the seller
   > Profit is limited to the credit, while the loss can be as large as the strike minus the premium if the stock goes to zero.

3. {#q3} What should you have available when selling a put?
   - Nothing at all, since put options are always settled in cash and never require the seller to buy shares
   - A long position in the stock, because a short put can only be sold against shares that are already owned
   - A long call at a higher strike, because the broker will not allow a put to be sold without one
   - [x] Enough cash or margin to buy the shares if you are assigned
   > Assignment obliges the seller to buy the stock at the strike, so the capital must be available.

4. {#q4} A trader sells a put and the stock finishes above the strike at expiration. What happens?
   - [x] The put expires worthless and the trader keeps the premium
   - The trader must buy 100 shares at the strike
   - The trader loses the premium that was collected, because the put was not exercised by the buyer
   - The trader owes the buyer the difference between the strike and the stock price at expiration
   > If the stock stays above the strike, the option is out of the money and the seller keeps the credit.
