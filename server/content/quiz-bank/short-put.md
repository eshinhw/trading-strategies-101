# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A trader builds a Short Put: sells one $100 put, for a net credit of $4 per share. What is the maximum profit at expiration, per share?
   - $96
   - $5
   - $3
   - [x] $4
   > For this position (K = 100, net credit = 4), the maximum profit is $4 per share. Formula: P_max = C.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A trader builds a Short Put: sells one $100 put, for a net credit of $4 per share. What is the break-even stock price at expiration, per share?
   - $4
   - [x] $96
   - $97
   - $95
   > For this position (K = 100, net credit = 4), the break-even stock price is $96 per share. Formula: S* = K - C.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A trader builds a Short Put: sells one $100 put, for a net credit of $4 per share. At expiration the stock is at $92. What is the trader's profit or loss per share, counting the premium?
   - +$4
   - −$8
   - −$3
   - [x] −$4
   > At $92 the option legs are worth −$8 per share before the premium, and the net premium adds +$4, for −$4 per share (given K = 100, net credit = 4).

4. {#bk4} [calc] A trader builds a Short Put: sells one $100 put, for a net credit of $4 per share. At expiration the stock is at $103. What is the trader's profit or loss per share, counting the premium?
   - [x] +$4
   - −$4
   - +$12
   - +$5
   > At $103 the option legs are worth $0 per share before the premium, and the net premium adds +$4, for +$4 per share (given K = 100, net credit = 4).

5. {#bk5} [calc] A trader builds a Short Put: sells one $100 put, for a net credit of $4 per share. What is the maximum loss on 8 contracts (100 shares each), in dollars?
   - $768
   - $9,600
   - [x] $76,800
   - $86,400
   > The maximum loss is $96 per share. One contract covers 100 shares, so 8 contracts give $96 × 100 × 8 = $76,800.
