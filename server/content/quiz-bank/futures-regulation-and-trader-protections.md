# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] An exchange position limit is 10,000 contracts and a trader holds 7,200. How many more contracts can be added?
   - 10,000
   - 7,200
   - 17,200
   - [x] 2,800
   > 10,000 − 7,200 = 2,800.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A trader holds 7,500 contracts and wants to add 40% more. The position limit is 10,000. By how much would the trader exceed the limit?
   - 0 contracts
   - [x] 500 contracts
   - 3,000 contracts
   - 2,500 contracts
   > A 40% addition is 3,000 contracts, for 10,500 in total. That is 500 above the limit.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A position limit is 10,000 contracts and open interest is 400,000. What fraction of open interest can one trader hold?
   - 25%
   - 0.25%
   - 4%
   - [x] 2.5%
   > 10,000 / 400,000 = 2.5%.

4. {#bk4} What does a national derivatives regulator such as the CFTC do?
   - [x] Oversees exchanges and clearinghouses, sets rules for handling customer funds, and investigates fraud and manipulation
   - Sets the daily settlement price of every contract
   - Guarantees every trader against trading losses
   - Matches buyers and sellers on exchange floors
   > The regulator sits above the exchange and clearinghouse, protecting customers against fraud and misuse of funds.

5. {#bk5} What does an exchange do as a self-regulatory organization?
   - It only provides a trading screen with no rulebook
   - It guarantees traders against price risk
   - [x] It writes and enforces its own rules, including contract specifications, position limits and discipline of members
   - It sets national tax rates on futures gains
   > Exchanges are the front-line regulator of their own markets.

6. {#bk6} Which risk does the regulatory structure NOT remove for a futures trader?
   - [x] Ordinary market risk from price movements and leverage
   - Fraud by a broker
   - Default by another trader
   - Unsuitable product sales
   > Regulation protects against fraud, counterparty default and unsuitable sales. It cannot stop prices from moving against a leveraged position.
