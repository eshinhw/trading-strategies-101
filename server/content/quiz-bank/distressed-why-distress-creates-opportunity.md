# Quiz

1. {#q1} What is a common cause of corporate distress?
   - Having no debt
   - [x] Too much debt combined with falling revenue or an industry downturn
   - Rising profits
   - Paying dividends
   > Heavy debt and weakening earnings make it hard to meet obligations.

2. {#q2} Why might the price of a distressed bond fall below what it could eventually recover?
   - Because all holders believe it is worthless
   - [x] Forced and fearful selling, such as institutions restricted from holding defaulted or low-rated debt
   - Because the company is profitable
   - Because it is guaranteed by the government
   > Selling that isn't based on the debt's value can push prices lower than the likely recovery.

3. {#q3} Why can distressed debt stay cheap even for investors who see value?
   - It cannot be analyzed
   - [x] Analyzing it takes legal and financial expertise, so fewer buyers are willing to do the work
   - It is illegal to buy
   - It always recovers fully
   > Complexity limits the number of buyers, which helps keep prices low.

4. {#q4} A distressed bond bought at 400 dollars later recovers 600 dollars. What is the gain?
   - 20%
   - 100%
   - [x] 50%
   - 200%
   > The gain is (600 − 400) / 400 = 50%.

5. {#q5} Why isn't a low price always a bargain?
   - A low price always means a bargain
   - [x] The market may be right that the debt is worth little, and recoveries are uncertain and can take years
   - Distressed debt cannot lose value
   - Courts guarantee full repayment
   > A discount may reflect real risk, so the investor must analyze the likely recovery.

6. {#calc1} [calc] An investor buys a $1,000 face-value bond for $350 and the eventual recovery is $520. What is the return?
   - 52%
   - [x] About 48.6%
   - About 17%
   - About 148.6%
   > The gain is $520 − $350 = $170, and $170 / $350 ≈ 48.6%.

7. {#calc2} [calc] Forced selling drives a bond to 35 cents on the dollar, while an investor estimates a 55-cent recovery. What is the upside to that estimate?
   - About 36.4%
   - About 20%
   - About 157%
   - [x] About 57.1%
   > The gain is 20 cents on a 35-cent price, and 20 / 35 ≈ 57.1%.
