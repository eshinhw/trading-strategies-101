# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A trader builds a Short Straddle: sells one $100 call and sells one $100 put, for a net credit of $6 per share. What is the lower break-even stock price at expiration, per share?
   - $106
   - [x] $94
   - $6
   - $0
   > For this position (K = 100, net credit = 6), the lower break-even stock price is $94 per share. Formula: S*_up = K + C; S*_down = K - C.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A trader builds a Short Straddle: sells one $100 call and sells one $100 put, for a net credit of $6 per share. At expiration the stock is at $103. What is the trader's profit or loss per share, counting the premium?
   - −$3
   - [x] +$3
   - +$15
   - +$4
   > At $103 the option legs are worth −$3 per share before the premium, and the net premium adds +$6, for +$3 per share (given K = 100, net credit = 6).

5. {#bk5} [calc] A trader builds a Short Straddle: sells one $100 call and sells one $100 put, for a net credit of $6 per share. What is the maximum profit on 3 contracts (100 shares each), in dollars?
   - [x] $1,800
   - $18
   - $600
   - $2,400
   > The maximum profit is $6 per share. One contract covers 100 shares, so 3 contracts give $6 × 100 × 3 = $1,800.
