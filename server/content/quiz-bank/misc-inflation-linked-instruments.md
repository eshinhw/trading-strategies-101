# Quiz

1. {#q1} What does a nominal government bond's yield reflect, compared to an inflation-linked bond's yield?
   - [x] Time value, credit risk, and expected inflation all bundled together, versus a more purely real, inflation-adjusted return
   - The two yields always reflect identical compensation with no difference
   - Nominal bonds carry no compensation for time value at all
   - Inflation-linked bonds never actually adjust for inflation in practice
   > The fundamental distinction is that a nominal yield bundles inflation compensation in with everything else, while an inflation-linked bond strips that out through its principal adjustment.

2. {#q2} What does breakeven inflation measure?
   - [x] The yield gap between a nominal bond and an inflation-linked bond of the same maturity, used as a market-implied inflation estimate
   - The exact inflation rate a central bank has officially targeted
   - A fixed number that never changes regardless of market conditions
   - The credit spread between two corporate bonds
   > Breakeven inflation is derived directly from the pricing relationship between nominal and inflation-linked bonds of matching maturity.

3. {#q3} Is breakeven inflation a pure, frictionless read on inflation expectations alone?
   - [x] No — it's also affected by supply/demand imbalances and a liquidity premium specific to inflation-linked bonds
   - Yes, it reflects expected inflation with absolutely no other influence
   - Breakeven inflation has no relationship to inflation expectations at all
   - Breakeven inflation is set directly by government decree
   > Real-world frictions like liquidity premiums and supply/demand mean breakeven inflation is a useful but imperfect proxy for pure inflation expectations.

4. {#q4} How can breakeven inflation be traded as a relative-value approach?
   - [x] By positioning for the yield gap between the two markets to widen or narrow, independent of an outright interest-rate view
   - Breakeven inflation cannot be traded under any circumstances
   - Only by taking an outright directional bet on nominal interest rates
   - Only by physically trading a basket of consumer goods
   > Trading the gap itself, rather than the direction of either underlying yield alone, is what makes this a genuine relative-value approach.

5. {#q5} A nominal bond yields 4.5% and a matched inflation-linked bond yields 2%. How is the 2.5% breakeven inflation figure derived?
   - [x] By subtracting the inflation-linked bond's 2% yield from the nominal bond's 4.5% yield
   - By surveying a panel of professional economists directly
   - It is set annually by the government with no market input
   - By adding the two yields together and dividing by three
   > The straightforward yield-gap calculation (4.5% − 2% = 2.5%) is exactly how breakeven inflation is read directly off market pricing.

6. {#calc1} [calc] A 10-year nominal bond yields 4.8% and a comparable inflation-linked bond yields 2.1%. What is breakeven inflation?
   - 6.9%
   - 2.1%
   - 1.35%
   - [x] 2.7%
   > Breakeven inflation is the yield gap: 4.8% − 2.1% = 2.7% a year.

7. {#calc2} [calc] An inflation-linked bond yields a real 1.8% and the market's breakeven inflation is 2.6%. What does a nominal bond of the same maturity yield?
   - About 0.8%
   - [x] About 4.4%
   - About 4.7%
   - About 2.6%
   > The nominal yield is the real yield plus breakeven inflation: 1.8% + 2.6% = 4.4%.

8. {#bk1} [calc] A TIPS has a $1,000 principal and a 1.5% real coupon. Inflation adjusts the principal to $1,040. What is the annual coupon?
   - [x] $15.60
   - $15.00
   - $16.00
   - $1.56
   > The coupon is paid on the adjusted principal: 1.5% × $1,040 = $15.60.

9. {#bk2} [calc] A 5-year nominal bond yields 4.0% while a 5-year TIPS yields 1.8%. If inflation averages 2.5%, which would perform better, and by about how much per year?
   - The nominal bond, by about 0.3%
   - The TIPS, by about 2.2%
   - [x] The TIPS, by about 0.3%
   - They would perform equally
   > The breakeven is 4.0% − 1.8% = 2.2%. Inflation of 2.5% is 0.3% higher than that, so the TIPS beats the nominal bond by about 0.3% a year.
