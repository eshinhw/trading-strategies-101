# Quiz

1. {#q1} What problem does a stock index solve?
   - It sets the price of each stock
   - [x] It combines many stocks into one number that summarizes how a market or segment is doing
   - It decides which companies can list on an exchange
   - It guarantees a return to investors
   > An index condenses the moves of many stocks into a single, easy-to-follow number.

2. {#q2} Why can an index be compared over many years?
   - It never changes
   - It is set by the government
   - [x] It is calculated by the same rules each day, which gives a consistent measure over time
   - It only includes one stock
   > Consistent, rule-based calculation is what makes an index a reliable yardstick over long periods.

3. {#q3} [calc] A fund returns 8% while the relevant index returns 12%. What does the index comparison show?
   - The fund beat the market by 4 points
   - The fund and the index performed the same
   - [x] The fund lagged the market by 4 percentage points
   - The index is wrong
   > Against its benchmark, the fund underperformed by 12% − 8% = 4 percentage points.

4. {#q4} Which of these is built on an index?
   - A corporate bond
   - [x] Index funds, ETFs, and index futures and options
   - A savings account
   - A company's annual report
   > Index funds, ETFs, and index derivatives all rely on a clearly defined index for the basket they track or reference.

5. {#q5} Why do many investors choose to match an index instead of trying to beat it?
   - Indexes always earn more than any other investment
   - Matching an index is illegal for professionals
   - [x] Beating a market benchmark is hard, and index products deliver its return at low cost
   - Indexes cannot lose value
   > Because most managers have trouble consistently beating a benchmark, many investors prefer a low-cost product that simply delivers the index's return.

6. {#calc1} [calc] An investor puts $50,000 in an active fund returning 6% a year, and another $50,000 in an index fund returning 9% a year. After 10 years (annual compounding), about how much larger is the index fund balance?
   - [x] About $28,800
   - About $15,000
   - About $2,900
   - About $118,400
   > The index fund grows to $50,000 × 1.09^10 ≈ $118,368 and the active fund to $50,000 × 1.06^10 ≈ $89,542, a gap of about $28,800.
