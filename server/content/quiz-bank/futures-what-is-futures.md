# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A trader buys 6 E-mini S&P 500 contracts (multiplier $50) at 4,200, and the price later moves to 4,236. What is the profit or loss on the position?
   - +$219
   - +$1,825
   - −$10,950
   - [x] +$10,950
   > The move is 4,236 − 4,200 = 36.50 per unit, and 36.50 × 50 × 6 contracts = $10,950.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A trader buys 3 corn contracts (5,000 bushels each) at 5.80, and the price later moves to 6.15. What is the profit or loss on the position?
   - +$1.05
   - [x] +$5,250
   - +$1,750
   - −$5,250
   > The move is 6.15 − 5.80 = 0.35 per unit, and 0.35 × 5,000 × 3 contracts = $5,250.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A trader sells 3 gold futures (100 ounces each) at $2,010. The price falls to $1,985. What is the profit?
   - $750
   - $75,000
   - −$7,500
   - [x] $7,500
   > A short gains when the price falls: ($2,010 − $1,985) × 100 × 3 = $7,500.

4. {#bk4} [calc] A trader buys 2 E-mini S&P 500 futures (multiplier $50) at 4,350. The index falls to 4,310. What is the result?
   - [x] −$4,000
   - −$80
   - +$4,000
   - −$40,000
   > The move is −40 points. −40 × $50 × 2 = −$4,000.

5. {#bk5} [calc] A trader buys one crude oil future (1,000 barrels) at $80 and another trader buys a $80 call option for $3 per barrel. Oil rises to $86. How much more does the futures position earn than the option, in total dollars?
   - $6,000
   - $9,000
   - [x] $3,000
   - $0
   > The future earns $6 × 1,000 = $6,000. The call earns ($6 − $3) × 1,000 = $3,000. The difference is $3,000.

6. {#bk6} [calc] A trader buys one crude oil future (1,000 barrels) at $80 and another buys an $80 call for $3 per barrel. Oil falls to $74. How much more does the futures position lose than the option?
   - [x] $3,000
   - $6,000
   - $3 per barrel more than the premium
   - $0, because both lose the same
   > The future loses $6 × 1,000 = $6,000. The call loses only its $3,000 premium. The future loses $3,000 more.

7. {#bk7} What does buying a futures contract commit the buyer to?
   - Buying the asset only if the price is favourable on the delivery date
   - Paying a premium for the right to buy later without any obligation
   - [x] Buying the asset at the agreed price on the agreed date, or settling the difference in cash
   - Owning a share of the company that issued the asset
   > A futures buyer is obligated to transact at the agreed price. Unlike an option buyer, the buyer cannot walk away if the market moves against them.

8. {#bk8} How does a futures contract differ from an option?
   - A futures buyer can let the contract expire worthless at no cost
   - An option obligates both sides, while a futures contract obligates neither
   - A futures contract requires a premium that an option does not
   - [x] Both sides of a futures contract are obligated, while an option buyer has a right but no obligation
   > A futures contract binds both parties to the trade. An option buyer pays a premium for a right and can simply let the option expire.

9. {#bk9} What is the main difference between a futures contract and a forward contract?
   - A future is customized between two parties, while a forward is traded on an exchange
   - [x] A future is standardized and traded on an exchange, while a forward is customized and negotiated privately
   - A future has no obligation to buy or sell, while a forward does
   - A future can only be settled in cash, while a forward can only be delivered physically
   > Both promise a transaction at an agreed price later. What differs is how the deal is made and enforced: exchange-traded and standardized for futures, private and customized for forwards.
