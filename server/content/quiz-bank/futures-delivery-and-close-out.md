# Quiz

1. {#bk1} [calc] A trader is long 2 index futures at 4,500 (multiplier $50). The contract is cash-settled at a final value of 4,530. What is the cash received?
   - $300
   - $30
   - [x] $3,000
   - $450,000
   > The gain is 30 points × $50 × 2 = $3,000, paid in cash because no physical asset is delivered.

2. {#bk2} [calc] A trader rolls an index future by selling the expiring contract at 4,520 and buying the next at 4,552 (multiplier $50). What does the roll cost per contract?
   - [x] $1,600
   - $32
   - $160
   - $16,000
   > The next contract is 32 points higher. 32 × $50 = $1,600 per contract.

3. {#bk3} [calc] A trader rolls 5 long crude oil contracts (1,000 barrels each) in backwardation by selling the expiring one at $82.50 and buying the next at $81.90. What is the roll benefit?
   - $600
   - $300
   - [x] $3,000
   - −$3,000
   > The next contract is $0.60 cheaper. $0.60 × 1,000 × 5 = $3,000 in the trader's favour.

4. {#bk4} [calc] A wheat contract covers 5,000 bushels and a short is matched for delivery at a contract price of $6.40. What invoice amount does the short receive?
   - $6.40
   - [x] $32,000
   - $3,200
   - $320,000
   > Invoice = $6.40 × 5,000 = $32,000.

5. {#bk5} [calc] A trader buys 2 crude oil contracts at $78.00 and closes them out with an offsetting sale at $80.50. What is the profit?
   - $2,500
   - $50,000
   - $500
   - [x] $5,000
   > The gain is $2.50 × 1,000 × 2 = $5,000.

6. {#bk6} [calc] A trader who forgets to close a short gold contract is matched for delivery at $2,015 per ounce. The contract covers 100 ounces. What cash does the short receive for delivering?
   - $2,015
   - [x] $201,500
   - $20,150
   - $2,015,000
   > The short delivers 100 ounces at $2,015: $2,015 × 100 = $201,500.

7. {#bk7} How does a cash-settled futures contract end?
   - The seller delivers the physical asset to a warehouse
   - The exchange cancels the contract and refunds the margin
   - The buyer must find a new counterparty to take over the position
   - [x] It settles against an agreed reference price or index level and the difference is paid in cash
   > Cash-settled contracts such as stock index futures have no physical asset to hand over, so the final price difference is settled in cash.

8. {#bk8} What is rolling a futures position?
   - Taking physical delivery and then reselling the asset
   - Letting the contract expire and waiting for settlement
   - [x] Closing the expiring contract and opening an equivalent position in a later-dated contract
   - Doubling the position size in the same contract
   > A roll keeps continuous exposure without ever taking delivery. The price gap between the two contracts becomes a gain or cost of the roll.

9. {#bk9} Why do most retail traders close or roll before a physically-settled contract's delivery process begins?
   - [x] The delivery process is operationally demanding, requiring storage, transport and quality arrangements
   - Physical delivery is illegal for individual traders
   - Delivery always loses money for the long side
   - The exchange cancels all positions held through the first notice date
   > Arranging storage and transport for a physical commodity is demanding, so traders without a use for it exit before the exchange's notice process starts.
