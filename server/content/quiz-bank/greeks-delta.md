# Quiz

1. {#q1} What is the range of a call option's delta?
   - −1 to 0
   - [x] 0 to 1
   - 0 to 100
   - −100 to 100
   > A call gains when the stock rises, so its delta is between 0 and 1.

2. {#q2} About what delta does an at-the-money call have?
   - 0
   - 1
   - [x] About 0.50
   - −0.50
   > An at-the-money call has roughly even odds of finishing in the money, and a delta near 0.50.

3. {#q3} [calc] A call has a delta of 0.60. About how much should it gain if the stock rises 1 dollar?
   - 0.06
   - [x] About 0.60 per share
   - 6.00 per share
   - Nothing
   > Delta is the change in the option's price for a 1 dollar change in the stock.

4. {#q4} [calc] How many shares does one call contract with a delta of 0.40 behave like?
   - 4 shares
   - [x] About 40 shares
   - 400 shares
   - 100 shares
   > Position delta is 0.40 × 100 = 40 shares.

5. {#q5} [calc] A covered call combines 100 shares and a short call with a delta of 0.40. What is the net delta?
   - 140
   - 100
   - [x] 60
   - 40
   > The stock contributes +100 and the short call contributes −40, for a net delta of 60.

6. {#bk1} [calc] A call option has a delta of 0.55. If the stock rises $2, by about how much does the option price change?
   - +$2.00
   - +$0.55
   - +$3.64
   - [x] +$1.10
   > Option change ≈ delta × stock change = 0.55 × $2 = $1.10.

7. {#bk2} [calc] A put option has a delta of −0.40. If the stock falls $3, by about how much does the put price change?
   - −$1.20
   - [x] +$1.20
   - +$3.00
   - +$7.50
   > Put change ≈ −0.40 × (−$3) = +$1.20. A put gains when the stock falls.

8. {#bk3} [calc] A trader is short 10 call contracts (100 shares each) with a delta of 0.45. How many shares should be bought to be delta-neutral?
   - 1,000 shares
   - 45 shares
   - 550 shares
   - [x] 450 shares
   > The short calls have delta −0.45 × 1,000 = −450. Buying 450 shares offsets it.

9. {#bk4} [calc] A trader owns 20 call contracts (100 shares each) with a delta of 0.60 and is short 500 shares of the stock. What is the net position delta in shares?
   - [x] +700
   - +1,200
   - −500
   - +200
   > The calls give 20 × 100 × 0.60 = +1,200 shares of delta. Adding −500 for the short stock gives +700.
