## Credit Risk

Credit risk is the chance that a bond's issuer fails to make its interest or principal payments, known as default. It also includes the risk that the issuer's finances weaken enough that investors worry about default, which pushes the bond's price down even though the issuer is still paying. Government bonds from stable countries are treated as having very little credit risk, while corporate bonds carry more.

## Credit Ratings

Rating agencies such as S&P, Moody's, and Fitch grade issuers on a letter scale, from the strongest (AAA, or Aaa at Moody's) down to default. Bonds rated BBB- (Baa3 at Moody's) or higher are called investment grade, and those below are called high yield, or sometimes junk bonds. A lower rating signals a higher chance of default, so investors require more return for taking the risk.

## The Credit Spread

The credit spread is the extra yield a bond pays over a risk-free government bond of similar maturity:

$$
\text{credit spread} = \text{yield on corporate bond} - \text{yield on government bond}
$$

If a Treasury yields 4.0% and a corporate bond of the same maturity yields 5.5%, the spread is 1.5%, or 150 basis points. The spread is the market's price of credit risk, and it is usually larger for lower-rated issuers.

## What Moves Spreads

Spreads change with the issuer's finances, the outlook for the economy, and how willing investors are to take risk. In calm times spreads tend to narrow, and in a crisis they widen sharply as investors demand more compensation. Because a bond's price moves opposite to its yield, a widening spread lowers the price of the bond even if the government yield doesn't change.

## In Practice

A company's bond is trading with a 150 basis point spread over Treasuries. The company is then downgraded and the spread widens to 250 basis points, so the bond's yield rises by 1.00%, assuming Treasury yields are unchanged. The bond has a duration of about 5, so its price falls by roughly 5 × 1.00% = 5%. Nothing has defaulted, but the market has repriced the risk, and the bondholder has lost value.
