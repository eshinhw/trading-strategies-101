## The Borrower's Problem

Governments building roads and companies building factories often need sums far larger than any one lender is willing to provide. Borrowing from a single bank can also mean a limited amount, shorter terms, and conditions that change over time. A borrower that can instead reach many lenders at once can raise more money, for longer, on more predictable terms.

## Splitting a Loan into Securities

A bond turns one large loan into many small, standardized pieces. Each piece has a face value, such as 1,000 dollars, a stated interest rate, and a maturity date, and each is sold to a different investor. Because the pieces are identical and tradable, a lender doesn't have to wait for repayment: they can sell their bond to someone else if they need their money back sooner.

## Who Issues Bonds

Governments issue bonds to cover budget deficits and fund public projects, and national governments such as the U.S. Treasury are the biggest issuers. Cities and states issue municipal bonds to build schools, bridges, and water systems. Companies issue corporate bonds to build, expand, or refinance debt. Each issuer is borrowing for a different reason, which is part of why bonds differ so much in risk and yield.

## Why Borrowers Choose Bonds

Compared with a bank loan, a bond can raise much larger amounts and lock in a rate for many years. Compared with selling shares, a bond doesn't give up any ownership of the company or a share of its future profits: the lender is paid back what was promised and nothing more. In many tax systems, a company's interest payments are also a deductible expense, which makes borrowing cheaper than raising money by selling ownership.

## In Practice

A city needs 50 million dollars to build a water treatment plant. Instead of borrowing from a single bank, it issues 30-year municipal bonds in pieces of 5,000 dollars each. Thousands of investors across the country each lend a small part, the city pays interest every year and repays the face value at maturity, and the investors can sell their bonds to others if they need their money earlier. The bond market has allowed a small city to borrow from thousands of lenders at once.
