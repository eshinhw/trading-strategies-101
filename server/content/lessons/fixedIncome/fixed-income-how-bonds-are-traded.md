## An Over-the-Counter Market

Unlike stocks, most bonds don't trade on a central exchange. Investors buy and sell through dealers who quote a price at which they will buy (the bid) and a price at which they will sell (the ask), and trades are negotiated directly. Government bonds such as U.S. Treasuries trade very actively, but many corporate and municipal bonds trade only occasionally, which can make them harder to buy or sell at a fair price.

## Quoting Prices as a Percentage of Face Value

Bond prices are quoted per 100 of face value, not in dollars. A quote of 98.50 means the bond trades at 98.5% of its face value, so a bond with a face value of 1,000 dollars would cost 985 dollars. A quote above 100 means the bond trades at a premium, and below 100 at a discount.

## Accrued Interest: Clean and Dirty Prices

Coupons are paid only a couple of times a year, but the interest accrues every day. When a bond is sold between coupon dates, the buyer pays the seller the interest earned since the last coupon. The quoted price, called the clean price, doesn't include that interest, while the amount actually paid, the dirty price, does:

$$
\text{dirty price} = \text{clean price} + \text{accrued interest}
$$

$$
\text{accrued interest} = \text{coupon} \times \frac{\text{days since last coupon}}{\text{days in coupon period}}
$$

Conventions for counting days differ between markets, but the idea is the same everywhere.

## Liquidity and Trading Costs

A dealer earns the difference between the price it pays and the price it sells at, so the bid-ask spread is a cost to every investor. Spreads are narrow for heavily traded government bonds and wider for less liquid corporate and municipal bonds, so an investor who may need to sell before maturity should check how easily a bond can be sold.

## Example

An investor buys 100 of the manufacturer's bonds from a dealer.

- Face value: 100 × $1,000 = $100,000
- Coupon: 5%, so $25 per bond every six months
- Dealer quote: 98.50, so the clean price is $985 per bond
- Days into the coupon period: 60 of 180

**Accrued interest per bond**

$$
\$25 \times \frac{60}{180} = \$8.33
$$

**Dirty price per bond**

$$
\$985.00 + \$8.33 = \$993.33
$$

**Total paid for 100 bonds**

$$
100 \times \$993.33 = \boxed{\$99{,}333}
$$

- Clean price: $98,500
- Accrued interest: $833.33

**At the next coupon**

- Coupon received: 100 × $25 = $2,500
- Repays the previous owner: $833.33
- Kept by the investor: $1,666.67 for the 120 days held

**Dealer spread cost (bid 98.30, ask 98.50)**

$$
0.20\% \times \$100{,}000 = \$200
$$

Buying at the ask and selling straight back at the bid loses $200.
