## Too Many Stocks to Watch

There are thousands of listed stocks, and each one's price changes throughout the day. If someone asks how the stock market did today, the honest answer would be a list of thousands of separate moves, which is impossible to take in. An index solves that by combining a chosen group of stocks into one number that rises and falls with the group.

## A Yardstick for the Market

An index tracks the performance of a market or of a slice of it, such as large U.S. companies, technology firms, or small companies. Because it is calculated by the same rules every day, it gives a consistent measure that can be compared over years or decades. When news reports say the market is up 1% on the day, they are almost always quoting a major index such as the S&P 500, the Dow Jones Industrial Average, or the Nasdaq-100.

## A Benchmark for Investors

An index also gives investors a standard to judge performance against. A fund manager who earned 8% in a year can't tell whether that was good without knowing what the market did, and comparing the result to a relevant index answers the question. This is the idea behind the phrase "beating the market", and it is also why many investors simply choose to match an index rather than try to beat it.

## A Foundation for Products

Because an index is a clearly defined, rule-based basket, products can be built on it. Index funds and ETFs hold the stocks in the index to deliver its return at low cost, and index futures and options let investors hedge or speculate on the whole market in a single trade. None of these would work without a transparent, widely accepted index to refer to, and they are covered later in this course.

## Example

An actively managed fund returns 8% in a year when the S&P 500 returns 12%. Without the index, 8% might look like a good result, but the comparison shows the manager lagged the market by 4 percentage points. An investor who had put the same money into a low-cost index fund tracking the S&P 500 would have earned close to 12% before fees, which is why indexes are used both to measure performance and to build products that simply deliver it.
